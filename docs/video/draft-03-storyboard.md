## Three personal-brief cases

1. **Needs attention:** an email announces a dispute-feed change. The Peer connects
   it to the Chargebacks mapper and prepares the migration guide and affected code.
2. **Worth joining:** office hours cover results that change later, matching an open
   validation check. The Peer drafts a specific question for Manish to review.
3. **Worth exploring:** a company hackathon's agent track matches a handoff idea
   Manish explicitly saved. The Peer prepares a draft pitch; the film shows Manish
   saving it for later. No registration or external sending is implied.

Each source is visible, explains **why this matters to Manish**, and resolves into
a usable next step. This is personal relevance, not an undifferentiated news feed.

## Conversation and pacing

The existing Chargebacks thread is kept as a recognizable through-line, but
spoken copy is shorter. The 2 a.m. explanation occurs in Slack only. Alex's Zoom
question directly addresses “Manish's Peer.” Human meeting tiles are camera-off,
and a screen share shows the build status, open checks and Slack evidence.

Sarah offers two test examples; she does not complete validation. A new mapping
attachment visibly arrives in Slack before the film calls it received. The next
brief leaves validation follow-up and Friday's launch decision with Manish.

The meeting turns have 350ms gaps. Voice takes were selected for concise natural
phrasing, with all Zoom speech at original speed. The score is newly composed at
124 BPM with scene-aligned beats, a deliberate opening intake, stronger reveal,
and speech ducking. Subjective sound quality was not verified by direct listening.
