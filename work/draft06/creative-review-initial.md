## Core recommendation

Give every chapter **one visible problem, one useful Peer action and one plain
payoff**. A title card alone will not fix the earlier difficulty understanding
the impact. Let the viewer see what would otherwise wait or be overlooked, then
see the specific change the Peer makes.

Four chapters form a coherent arc: the Peer notices a gap for Manish, helps a
colleague while he sleeps, contributes in a delegated meeting, then brings him
the important things he missed. The personal benefit remains visible throughout.

## Opening: earn the emotional change

Show a short, recognizably calm beginning: employee walks in, sits, opens the
laptop and starts work with a small smile. Allow the first useful work beat to
land before the first alert. Eyes respond first, then shoulders and posture
change as demands accumulate. Avoid starting with the character already worried.

The attention circle remains a useful organizing device. At the reveal, let the
Peer receive routine information while one meaningful work item remains with
the employee. This makes “focus where you're needed most” an observable result.
Avoid suggesting the Peer simply deletes all requests.

## Voice and pacing guidance

The narrator can be warm and confident; colleagues should sound matter-of-fact,
with modest pauses and contractions. The Peer should sound calm and helpful.
Avoid making normal clarifications sound like an excited product announcement.
Voices should be selected for roles, not imitations of real colleagues.

Keep one idea per utterance, explain the business need before field names,
and reveal UI in the same order as the spoken explanation. Do not keep the old
duration by accelerating long new lines. A clear four-minute story is preferable
to a shorter stream of unexplained jargon.
