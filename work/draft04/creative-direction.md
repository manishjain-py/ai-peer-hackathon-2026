Read: project instructions, current state, Draft 03 storyboard, overview, exact

Draft 04 exists; it is not a rendered-video approval.

## The idea that should lead the film

**A person should not have to be the connection between everything.**

Manish's circle idea is stronger than Draft 03's source cards because it gives
the audience someone to feel for. The emotional progression should be
concentration → interruption → divided attention → relief → agency. The promise
is choosing where his attention goes, supported by a Peer that knows enough to
help. It is larger than being available while asleep.

The biggest creative risk is implementing this as a decorative notification
wheel. The human must cause the audience to understand the problem. Their gaze,
posture and interrupted work should change at every beat.

Timing can move by a few seconds. Preserve the order of emotional beats and keep
the personal brief as the first demonstration after setup.

## Narration discipline

- Narration should explain the feeling and promise. The UI can carry the numbers.
  Avoid reading “150 emails, ten pings, three meetings” as an inventory.
- Strong opening territory: “One person. A hundred places your attention needs to
  be.” Then something like: “The routine questions. The change you can't miss.
  The work you actually wanted to do.” Keep language human and concrete.
- Reveal territory: “What if you had a Peer who knew your work?” followed by
  “To keep you in the loop. And help with the things that don't need to wait.”
- Product promise should explicitly return attention to Manish. Existing
  “Your work. A step ahead.” can remain a brand line, but the opening needs the
  more direct emotional explanation: “More room for what needs you.”
- Do not use “clone,” “knows everything,” “handles it all,” or “never miss
  anything.” A peer works within delegated access and has evidence.

## Motion and sound direction

1. Start individual notification arrivals about 2.5 seconds apart; tighten the
   interval near the peak. Acceleration conveys pressure without fast speech.
2. Keep the human's face above the caption region. Early cards land in the
   direction of gaze, not in arbitrary screen space.
3. Use three layers: person/task in the foreground, readable hero cards in the
   middle, small accumulated pulses in the background. Only one new hero card
   should demand reading at a time.
4. Hold 150 unread and two late meeting times visibly. A beautiful animation
   that never lets these facts be read fails the user's request.
5. The circle-to-Peer opening is the signature transition. Do not spin the entire
   view like a loading indicator or send the human into a vortex.
6. Let the same small path/particle become an extraction highlight during setup,
   then a source chip in the personal brief. This creates a single connected film.
7. Sound should be selective: two distinct notification timbres, a muted pressure
   layer, a brief intake of quiet, then a warm upbeat reveal. Ten pings should not
   mean ten loud effects. Existing upbeat music can return at the release.

## Issues to fix if they appear in execution

**Blocking creative issues:** the figure is merely a static avatar; the first
twenty seconds have no emotional escalation; all demand cards disappear before
Peer arrives; setup is just a logo montage; personal relevance is pushed behind
Slack/Zoom; text at peak is unreadable; “150 unread” and late-night invites are
never actually shown; brand language implies connected production integrations.

**High-value polish:** the same useful task starts dimming as requests accumulate
and brightens again at release; the calendar overlap is visual, not only verbal;
the cursor press produces a satisfying motion match; profile facts visibly turn
into a work map; the work map's Chargebacks node becomes the first brief's code
context. Bring the seated person back for 1–2 seconds near the closing payoff so
the film ends with the benefit established at its beginning.

## Review checklist for the eventual render

- Can a viewer identify the person and their interrupted work within two seconds?
- Can the gaze/reaction sequence be understood without the narration?
- Are the count of 150 emails, growth to 10 Slack pings and three invites/two
  nighttime meetings readable at normal playback?
- Does the reveal visibly give attention back to the person?
- Is creation clear, and are One Airbnb, Sourcegraph, GitHub, Slack and Zoom each
  used for a distinct, understandable role?
- Is the company/source material visibly illustrative rather than a claim of
  actual internal access?
- Does source learning explain the personalized brief that follows?
- Does the film retain both benefits: what Manish needs to know and how the Peer
  helps others?
- Do the human's consequential decisions remain with him?
