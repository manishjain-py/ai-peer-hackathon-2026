If all three orbit rapidly at once, the problem becomes spectacle without meaning. The typography “One person. Too many places to be.” can be the final synthesis, not a fourth competing message.

**Make the reveal a release of pressure.** At 16–18 seconds, converge the incoming/outgoing lines, briefly remove the beat, then reveal AI Peer on the next downbeat. Leave the title large and stable long enough to register; the two value paths should form around it. Avoid a generic logo zoom followed by an unrelated source slide.

**Use one recurring source object.** A small blue source chip begins as the relevant email line, becomes a brief item, and later the related Slack evidence enters Zoom. Each transfer should preserve a readable label and color. This gives the film causal motion rather than a string of fades.

**Animate relevance as a discovery.** For each personal brief item, reveal the raw sentence first, then connect it to “your service,” “your open check,” or “your saved idea.” Only then resolve into the suggested next action. The third item should feel like a small delight: an overlooked opportunity becomes personally useful. Show Manish save the pitch rather than imply automatic registration or sending.

**Treat the meeting as a real quick sync.** Camera-off participant tiles plus shared evidence are credible. Keep the AI label visible from the opening meeting frame. Start the human's question quickly after entry, use 0.3–0.6-second conversational turn gaps, and let evidence respond to the specific spoken point. Do not cycle slides merely because a speaker changes.

**Make the return visibly changed.** Reuse the personal-brief shell and animate “New since your last brief.” Two source-linked updates arrive; one decision rises to the top. This closes the loop and proves continuity. It must not become an unrelated summary UI.
