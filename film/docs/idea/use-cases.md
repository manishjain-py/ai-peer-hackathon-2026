## Two directions of value

Manish’s 18 September clarification makes the employee’s own use a central
part of the product. The Peer watches permitted sources for relevant changes,
priorities, events and opportunities, as well as helping other people obtain
context. A daily brief is personalized by actual work and explicitly saved
interests, not merely a chronological inbox digest.

The earlier Draft 03 examples remain preserved in that film's storyboard.

Each item should show the source, why it matters to this employee and a concrete
next action. Drafting is distinct from sending, registering, committing or
approving. These are proposed behaviors and illustrative film states.

## The collaboration story

An engineer needs a field from another team's dataset. The owning team asks
for the use case while the engineer is asleep. The Peer supplies approved
project context with a source, allowing the owning engineer to recommend the
right dataset/API during their working day. The employee returns to a concise
record and any unresolved decisions.

The useful outcome is a clarification completed without another timezone
round trip. The Peer does not approve the recommendation, invent missing
requirements, or silently commit the employee to an implementation decision.

## Scenarios

| Scenario | Intended Peer contribution | Required evidence or boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Offline project question | Give the latest supported status and flag follow-up | Current authoritative source; clear AI identity |
| Cross-time-zone clarification | Explain the employee's requirement so a colleague can answer | Relevant design context, permitted recipients, no invented commitment |
| Missed meeting | Attend as an identified delegate, provide supported context, capture questions | Explicit delegation; meeting participation rules and scoped knowledge |
| Meeting handoff | Separate decisions, questions, answers, and items needing review | Traceable conversation record; no claim an unconfirmed date is approved |
| Historical project question | Explain why an approach was selected or rejected | Link requirements, discussions, decisions, and implementation |
| Engineering assistance | Explain ownership, investigate context, propose plans or changes | Scoped repository access; write/merge permissions handled separately |
| Onboarding | Explain systems from a team's perspective | Recipient access checks; cite source material |
| PTO coverage | Answer routine context questions and escalate exceptions | Employee-defined boundaries and a reviewable activity feed |
| Peer-to-Peer collaboration | Exchange routine context between authorized delegates | Future exploration, not a first-demo implementation claim |

## Demo through-line

1. **Protect focus:** a fictional photographic employee is already seated and
   happy; accumulating requests change his expression before the Peer brings relief.
2. **Create and understand:** set up the Peer with profile, team, projects, GitHub,
   proposed AirChat local-work context and chosen communication sources.
3. **Connect company news:** relate the BOBO launch to payments-risk responsibilities
   and identify a possible involvement gap and product-owner contact action.
4. **Continue across time zones:** while Manish sleeps, an identified Peer explains
   the Synthetic Cancellation data requirement so the US owner can offer guidance.
5. **Exchange meeting context:** generic Maya/Product, Alex/D-REX and Jordan/Case
   Platform notice missing RPS representation. The Peer identifies itself and
   steps in because it is late for Manish. It gives draft status, requests a fresh
   case with disputed-product amounts, listens to partner updates and records
   owners and next steps after the relevant speech. Launch review stays with Manish.
6. **Recover what matters:** surface illustrative personal messages, a relevant
   invitation and a Cassette dependency update, with source previews and useful
   choices. A personalized daily brief brings these signals and follow-ups together.
7. **Return a handoff:** preserve useful context and separate open questions and
   consequential decisions for the employee's review.

Draft 07 uses a distinct full-screen title before each use case and preserves

## Activity feed requirements

Each entry should distinguish the incoming request, the Peer response, sources,
and outcome. Show the actor as the Peer, not the employee. A recommendation
received from a colleague is not automatically a task completed by the Peer.
The film may show a resolved information dependency as an illustrative outcome.

## Evidence to gather in a later prototype

- Can a colleague get a correct, useful clarification without waiting for the employee?
- Can the employee understand and correct the interaction on returning?
- Does the Peer decline or escalate unsupported, stale, or consequential requests?
- Can source access and recipient permissions be preserved during representation?
