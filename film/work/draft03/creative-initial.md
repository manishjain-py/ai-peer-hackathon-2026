# Draft 03 — independent initial creative direction

18 September 2026. Reviewed Draft 02 storyboard, exact dialogue, verification and independent reviews against Manish's latest feedback. This is direction for a new script, not approval of a finished film.

## Superseding direction: equal value for Manish himself

Manish's additional feedback expands the film's core promise. Give personal relevance and proactive daily preparation equal emphasis with representing him to others. **The recommendations below about only three tiny extra vignettes are superseded:** the daily brief is now a main scene, not a quick feature montage.

Suggested opening: **“A change in your service. A deadline in your inbox. A teammate waiting on your answer. All relevant. All at once.”** Show information flowing toward Manish from approved email, Slack and company events, while requests flow back out. The problem is divided attention and scattered relevance, not just somebody being offline.

Two simple promises: **“Know what matters. Help work move.”** Candidate close: **“Stay informed. Stay involved.”** Another viable brand line is “Your world of work. Working for you.” Both fit the employee's own priorities and useful representation; the earlier expertise-only line is now too narrow.

Use one workday as the narrative: morning brief helps Manish decide where to focus; later, Slack and a delegated Zoom meeting help his collaborators; the next handoff returns control to him. Daily brief cards must each show **source → why relevant to Manish → useful next action**, with three distinct examples: a change affecting a service/project he owns, a time-sensitive priority, and an AI hackathon or learning opportunity matching his work and interests. This demonstrates understanding, rather than a generic inbox digest. Avoid reading every source/title aloud.

Do not let the character become merely a passive recipient: show Manish choose a priority, save a relevant event, or open the affected project. Routine representation still needs one source-backed useful answer and one decision retained by him.

## The central creative change

The film should sell **the value of a person's expertise in more places**, with availability as one benefit. Draft 02 spends too long establishing permission, absence and mechanical handoffs; the viewer waits to see a useful answer. Make each scene reveal something new: Slack answers a precise question, Zoom carries fresh information into a decision, and the final montage shows preparation, onboarding and resolving contradictory updates.

Suggested end line: **“Your expertise. Wherever work happens.”** This accommodates the employee being busy, preparing for work or bringing somebody up to speed. Avoid another timezone tagline after a timezone-led Slack scene.

## Opening and reveal

Open with three concrete questions arriving in different surfaces over 8–10 seconds: “Which source is current?” / “What's still blocking Friday?” / “Why did we choose this?” Each needs the same person. Show the small consequences: a paused answer, a meeting waiting, a new teammate without the backstory. Avoid a giant abstract clock for 20 seconds.

Narration option: **“The work is shared. The backstory often isn't. So a quick question becomes a wait. A meeting becomes a recap. And the same explanation starts again.”** Compress the three questions into one compact Peer mark on a clear musical downbeat. Reveal: **“Meet AI Peer. Your expertise, ready to help.”**

Then show just enough source setup to make the answer believable. One fast, readable chain: selected project notes → a newer update replaces an old one → “Routine answers / Team updates / Decisions stay with you.” Do not narrate every label. Spend the saved time on useful participation.

## Meeting rewrite and naturalness

Go directly to **“Manish's Peer, what's still blocking Friday?”** The AI's name and badge already identify it; no roll call, no second 2 a.m., no elaborate absence apology. A second participant can briefly say, “I can see his Peer here,” only if needed, but it costs momentum without adding information.

Use spoken turns, not readout paragraphs: **“The dashboard's built. We still need two checks: a duplicate event, and a result that changes later.”** Follow with a real discovery: **“And Sarah just confirmed the hourly source in Slack.”** A small source chip travels from Slack to the shared Zoom evidence; it should feel like the same fact arriving, not a new slide.

Let human turns land about **0.25–0.7 seconds** apart. Draft 02's typical 1.8–2.5 second turn gaps explain much of its slowness. Preserve one intentional short pause before the reveal; remove habitual dead air. Do not simply speed up existing synthetic speech.

If lip-synced webcam footage is unavailable, use honest camera-off profile tiles and screen share. Photoreal frozen faces plus new voices will still look unlike a real meeting.

## Pace, music and the extra use cases

Aim for **165–185 seconds**, roughly 350–400 spoken words with purposeful reading holds. This is a pacing target, not a requirement to cram speech. Keep the primary Slack/Zoom story. Add at most three **5–7 second** vignette beats after it: explain a past decision to a new teammate; prepare Manish for his next meeting; catch a changed requirement before an old answer is repeated. These should show one question and one useful result each, not merely capability names.

An upbeat 112–120 BPM instrumental can support lively transitions. Save a small drop for “Meet AI Peer,” duck around dialogue, and let the close regain energy. It must be an actual musical phrase with a finish, not a metronomic synth loop that ends abruptly. Voice naturalness and subjective music quality need listening; ASR and loudness checks cannot certify them.

## Review standard for the next pass

Ask whether an unfamiliar viewer can repeat the problem and the product after 25 seconds. Every scene must show a different benefit. The final review must judge dramatic momentum, clarity, voice believability and music—not only whether text is clipped. Preserve clear AI identity, source evidence, human ownership of commitments, and the illustrative/concept disclosure.

## TLDR

Lead with repeated questions and missing backstory, reveal the Peer decisively, and make the meeting sound like a real short exchange. Show expertise helping beyond offline hours. Faster turn-taking, connected objects and a properly shaped upbeat soundtrack matter more than adding more effects.
