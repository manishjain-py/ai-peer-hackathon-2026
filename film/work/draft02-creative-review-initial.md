# AI Peer Draft 02 — independent creative review

Date: 18 September 2026. Review basis: Draft 01 storyboard, actual Draft 01 contact sheet, current state, product/trust documents, production template. This is a creative review, not evidence of full motion playback or direct audio listening.

## Diagnosis

Draft 01 explains a feature list clearly, but does not let the audience *experience* the problem or the relief. Almost every frame uses the same grammar: headline above a small white card. The meeting is effectively one more card. The audience is told that context is available without witnessing an awkward pause, a realistic exchange, or a consequential question resolved.

The clean typography and controlled palette are useful foundations. The weaknesses are structural, not just missing visual effects:

- The setup occupies nearly half the movie before a useful conversation happens.
- The team interface is a generic chat shell; the meeting interface is a generic participant panel. Neither feels like an application people use daily.
- "What's the use case?" and "authoritative dataset" demand too much inference. Use one understandable question and answer, then show exactly what the colleague can do next.
- The Peer is introduced repeatedly through headings instead of through its behavior.
- The work example loses continuity at the meeting. It is not obvious whether the meeting advances the same dependency.
- The small UI competes with large explanatory headlines. The product should become the visual hero in the middle.
- Flat cartoon avatars and identical centered panels create a slide-deck impression. Adding more easing to those panels would not fix the impression.

## Creative proposition

**The working day ends. The conversation doesn't have to.**

Make the absence tangible. Start inside a real-feeling Slack workspace, build one unresolved question, then reveal the person asleep on the other side of the world. Show the Peer solving the small Slack question, carrying the newly learned answer into a Zoom meeting, and returning the whole conversation to Manish the next morning.

The magical act is not a glowing AI network. It is the precise moment a conversation that should have stopped continues—with an answer, evidence, and a clear limit.

Target 3:20–3:40. Do not stretch to four minutes unless conversations need the space. Roughly 360–440 spoken words including dialogue; actual synthesis durations must determine the final timing.

## One specific story

Project: **Premium Chargebacks pilot**. A team needs to know which dispute events belong in the pilot, then whether the rollout is ready. Manish owns the requirements and readiness decision. Every source and result is illustrative.

Shared facts, repeated consistently in Slack, Zoom and handoff:

1. The pilot needs the *final outcome* of a dispute before reconciliation.
2. Sarah from Data Platform supplies the authoritative dataset/API answer.
3. Development is complete; QA still has one scenario to validate.
4. The launch decision remains with Manish.

Use only two necessary technical labels in the film: "final dispute outcome" and "Events API". The story should still make sense if the viewer knows nothing about chargebacks.

## Suggested film, including dialogue

### 0:00–0:26 — Hook: one small question, half a world away

Begin almost silent, with a beautiful tight shot of Slack's message composer. A blinking cursor; a few words arrive; message sends.

**Sarah, Slack:** "@Manish quick one: do you need every dispute update, or just the final outcome?"

The sent time is 1:30 PM. We pull back from the message into a convincingly detailed Slack workspace: channel list, profile thumbnails, date separator, tiny edited/reaction elements, sidebar density. Sarah's avatar is active. Manish's profile opens briefly: "Local time 2:00 AM · Notifications paused."

**Jordan, Slack:** "He's offline in Bengaluru. We'll have to pick this up tomorrow."

Freeze the blinking waiting cursor as a thin line. That line becomes the divide between a warm afternoon workspace and a sleeping city at night. Two clocks lock together: **San Francisco · 1:30 PM / Bengaluru · 2:00 AM**. Mark the different calendar day accurately.

**Narrator:** "A small question. Half a world away. Sometimes work stops—not because the answer is hard, but because the person who knows it is offline."

Headline, two beats: **The person is offline. / What if the context wasn't?**

Sound: one notification, then the music briefly drops away during the waiting beat. Leave one full second of uncomfortable stillness; it is the joke and the problem.

### 0:26–0:47 — The reveal: AI Peer

The idle indicator turns into the Peer mark through a restrained shape transformation. The Slack window gently folds into a bright product stage; the title arrives on a musical lift.

**Narrator:** "Meet AI Peer. A personal AI teammate that understands your work, and can keep routine conversations moving when you can't be there."

Use a quiet, readable **Concept film · illustrative scenarios** label. Keep a tiny "Concept demo" bug through the remainder, with the full disclosure again at the end. Do not let a legal-looking screen interrupt the story.

### 0:47–1:12 — Before the night: context and permission

Subtitle: **Earlier that evening**. This prevents the film from suggesting the Peer learns everything only after Manish is asleep.

Rather than showing four generic source cards, show one actual source excerpt: "The pilot requires the final dispute outcome before reconciliation." The line is selected, moves into a concise project brief, and retains its small document icon and timestamp. A status note joins it: "Development complete · Final QA scenario pending."

**Narrator:** "Give it the context you choose: the projects, conversations, and decisions behind your work. It keeps the source with the answer, and the boundaries you set."

Manish enables two exact controls: **Answer routine questions in #premium-chargebacks** and **Represent me in tomorrow's project sync**. Under them: **Launch decisions → ask me**.

Manish sets away. The camera follows his status dot into the overnight Slack view. No full reset of the composition.

### 1:12–1:54 — Slack: a real back-and-forth

Return to the same message, preserving scroll position. Typing indicator belongs to **Manish's AI Peer**, with Slack's APP badge. Do not type a paragraph slowly; let one short sentence appear, hold it, then the next.

**Peer, Slack:** "Manish is offline. I'm his AI Peer. The pilot needs the final dispute outcome before reconciliation. Which source should we use for that?"

Source chip beneath: **Pilot requirements · updated today**. Click briefly exposes the exact matching sentence, not a decorative document title.

**Sarah:** "Use dispute_events through the Events API. It includes the final outcome."

**Peer:** "Got it. I'll include the source and your recommendation in Manish's handoff."

**Sarah:** "Perfect. I can finish the integration plan."

The exchange has one specific question and an actionable outcome. Give each major message a 4–6 second readable hold at the tight framing. Leave Slack controls and adjacent conversation visible so it still feels like Slack, not floating message balloons.

**Narrator, after the exchange:** "One clarification. A useful answer. And a next step that no longer has to wait."

Transition: Sarah's "integration plan" message becomes a pinned meeting agenda item, then pulls into the Zoom screen-share region. One physical object connects the applications.

### 1:54–2:48 — Zoom: the exact moment absence becomes presence

Open on a convincing Zoom gallery with three human participants, webcam-like lighting and room backgrounds, familiar name bars, toolbar, meeting title and a clearly nonhuman Peer tile. No black rectangle pretending to be a functioning human video. Peer tile label: **Manish's AI Peer · AI**. It is quiet until addressed. Source/status content appears as meeting chat or shared brief, not a giant subtitle panel laid over everyone's faces.

**Jordan:** "Before we move on—Manish, are we ready for the Premium Chargebacks pilot?"

Beat. Gallery holds. Subtle ambient room tone; music drops under live dialogue.

**Sarah:** "I don't think Manish could join. It's the middle of the night for him."

**Peer:** "Hi, I'm Manish's AI Peer. He's asked me to join on his behalf. I can share his latest update."

Zoom active-speaker treatment moves to the Peer tile. A small waveform reacts precisely to its speech. The camera gently pushes in; surrounding tiles remain recognizable.

**Peer:** "Development is complete. One QA scenario is still pending. Sarah has now confirmed the data source, so the integration plan can move ahead."

On-screen brief: **Development: complete / QA: one scenario pending / Data source: confirmed by Sarah**. A source affordance opens to the same Slack message from the previous sequence. That is the payoff: the Peer carries a real conversation forward.

**Jordan:** "Great. So can we confirm the launch date?"

**Peer:** "That decision stays with Manish. I'll flag it for his morning review."

**Jordan:** "That helps. Let's finish QA and leave the launch call with him."

This is the strongest differentiating scene. Preserve human pauses and natural turn-taking. Never play narration on top of participant dialogue. Use distinct voices, no caricature accents, no cloned Manish voice.

### 2:48–3:17 — Morning: return the conversation, not a wall of logs

Night blue rolls to morning cream through the same clock/circle shape. **Bengaluru · 8:30 AM**. Manish opens a compact handoff with three items:

- **Slack:** Requirement clarified; Sarah confirmed Events API.
- **Zoom:** Shared readiness update; one QA scenario remains.
- **For you:** Confirm launch date after QA.

Selecting the Slack item reveals the actual exchange. Selecting Zoom briefly reveals the exact Peer statement. The review item is visually distinct; it is not marked complete.

**Narrator:** "When Manish returns, he gets the conversation, the sources, and the one decision that needs him. Less catching up. More moving forward."

Manish starts a reply: **"Thanks. I'll confirm after QA."** This closes the loop without inventing an unearned launch success.

### 3:17–3:30 — Close

The Slack reply, meeting brief, and review card align into one clean thread leading back to Manish. Each arrives on a separate musical note. Pull out to AI Peer wordmark.

**Narrator:** "You can't be everywhere. Your context can. AI Peer. Keep the conversation moving."

Final card, 4-second hold: **AI Peer / Keep the conversation moving.** Small readable line: **Hackathon concept · Illustrative Slack and Zoom scenarios · Product integrations not yet implemented.**

## Execution notes that matter more than extra effects

1. **Slack and Zoom must be instantly recognizable.** Match current layout, spacing, toolbars, badges, sidebar proportions, window dressing and text weight from official screenshots. Reconstruct for crisp animation rather than scaling a low-resolution screenshot. The content remains fictional and the concept label stays visible. Public reference screenshots are design references, not permission to pretend a live integration works.
2. **Put the UI in charge.** In the conversation sections, application windows should occupy around 85–95% of the frame. Establishing views may be wide; push into the useful detail, hold, then return to context.
3. **Each transition preserves one object.** Slack question → time-zone divider; source excerpt → project brief; away dot → night indicator; Slack recommendation → Zoom agenda; meeting statement → morning handoff. This is connected motion, not a slideshow with different transitions.
4. **Vary scale and rhythm.** First 25 seconds: several 2–4 second beats. Middle: 5–9 second exchanges. Meeting pause: nearly motionless. Payoff: calm, readable. Avoid constant floating/bobbing.
5. **Human realism must be coherent.** If participant images are stills, frame them as camera-off profile tiles or use subtle credible webcam motion. Beautiful but obviously static webcam faces damage the realism more than honest camera-off participants. Never animate an unrelated mouth underneath voiced dialogue.
6. **Sound carries the absence/presence reversal.** Music drops out when somebody asks for Manish; it returns gently after the Peer supplies value. Sparse clicks and application-like tones, never a sound for every card.
7. **Sources are used, not just displayed.** At least once show a clicked source revealing the exact supporting sentence. A row of unexplained citation chips is insufficient.
8. **Protect reading time.** Subtitle text cannot cover the Slack reply or Zoom speaker name. Long messages should be split, camera-framed, or spoken; never shrink them to make the layout pass.

## Independent review gates

### Script gate

- Can a nontechnical viewer describe the problem and value in one sentence?
- Is the Slack question concrete, and is its resolution used in the Zoom exchange?
- Does the meeting contain all four beats: ask Manish → notice absent → Peer identifies itself → supplies useful context?
- Does the viewer see where the Peer knows the information from?
- Does one decision visibly remain with Manish?
- Does the morning resolve the story rather than merely summarize features?
- Is every spoken line necessary, human, and comfortably timed?

### Video gate

- Within two seconds of each application reveal, can a viewer identify Slack/Zoom without being told?
- Can every essential message be read on a laptop at ordinary playback speed?
- Does the motion preserve spatial continuity and visibly direct attention?
- Is there actual dramatic timing: anticipation, pause, answer, relief?
- Do the voices feel like a conversation, with distinct speakers and no overlap?
- Does the app feel alive without inaccurate phantom controls or random cursor movement?
- Are dated/time-zone views internally consistent, including the previous day in San Francisco?
- Can the complete exported MP4 be understood with sound off, and does direct listening confirm a clear mix?
- Is the concept status clear without overwhelming the film?

## Biggest creative risk

The requested "magical" feel will not come from longer duration, more gradients, or more transitions. It will come from one expertly timed change: everyone assumes the conversation must stop; an openly identified AI carries the right context into the room; the human owner comes back to an accountable handoff. Build the movie around that moment.
