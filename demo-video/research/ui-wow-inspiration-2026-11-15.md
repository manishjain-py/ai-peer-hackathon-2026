# How the best products show an AI at work: inspiration for the AI Peer's "wow" screens (2026-11-15)

Research for WP6 (wow concepts for the AI Peer's own screens), by a read-only research helper of
ai-peer-video-wp6 with web search, on 15 November 2026. Generic only: public products and public
pages. About 45 references, nearly all primary (the makers' own pages); every item has its link and
one line on what to borrow. Used by the builders of the five concepts in `demo-video/ui-concepts/`.
Links were checked by search and page reads on the day; openai.com and Codrops refuse automated
readers, so those two links come from search results.

## 1. How the best products show an AI at work

**Setup: a visible plan, then learning**
- **Gemini Deep Research** ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products/gemini/tips-how-to-use-deep-research/)). It shows its research plan before it starts, with "Edit plan". While it works it offers "Show thinking" and "Sites browsed". Borrow: the four setup steps as a plan that ticks off, with a live list of sources beside it.
- **OpenAI deep research** ([launch post](https://openai.com/index/introducing-deep-research/)). A sidebar sums up the steps taken and the sources used. Borrow: a side rail where sources pile up as they are read.
- **Claude Research with Google Workspace** ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/research)). It runs searches "that build on each other" across Gmail, Calendar and Docs, with inline citations. This is the closest public match to our step 2.
- **Dia browser** ([Getting started](https://www.diabrowser.com/getting-started)). A five-chapter welcome tour. One chapter, "Learns how you work", says it "picks up on patterns: your projects, teammates, and priorities."
- **Claude memory** ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/memory)). It keeps "a memory summary … for you to view and edit." Borrow: "What I learned" as an open page you can edit.
- **Notion 3.0 Agent** ([Notion blog](https://www.notion.com/blog/introducing-notion-3-0)). Its instructions page is its memory, and you give it a name and accessories. It has a personality without a mascot taking over the screen.

**The brief**
- **ChatGPT Pulse** ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-pulse/)). It researches overnight and hands over skimmable visual cards each morning, with thumbs up or down and "Curate".
- **Google Labs CC** ([Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/google-labs/cc-ai-agent/)). A "Your Day Ahead" email split into "Top of Mind" and "FYI". You reply to it to teach it. Borrow: two levels of urgency.

**Sources being read, and citations**
- **Gemini in Chrome** ([Chrome help](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/16283624?hl=en)). A tab Gemini is using "shows a glowing underline", which goes away when you stop sharing it. Borrow: each source chip glows while it is being read.
- **Granola** ([launch post](https://www.granola.ai/blog/announcement)). "Everything from you is in black, everything else is in gray." "Zoom In" shows "the exact quotes the note is based on." Borrow: two inks, one for the person and one for the AI, plus a zoom from a fact to its quote.
- **NotebookLM** ([help: Use chat](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16179559?hl=en)). Hover a citation to see the full quote; click it to jump to that spot in the source.
- **Glean** ([citations docs](https://docs.glean.com/user-guide/assistant/glean-chat/glean-chat-citations/glean-citations)). Inline markers plus a "View sources" list. Hover previews highlight the quoted text. "Citations never grant new access."
- **C2PA Content Credentials** ([guidance for builders](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.0/ux/UX_Recommendations.html)). A small pin opens a summary with who signed it and the date, then full detail. Borrow: a layered source-and-date chip on every card.

**Character and light**
- **Mico**, Copilot's voice character ([PSYOP case study](https://psyop.com/work/microsoft-ai-mico)). A soft blob "defined not by fixed features, but by behavior", made to "follow your lead rather than interrupt it." Holding back is what keeps it charming.
- **Duolingo's Lily** ([Rive blog](https://rive.app/blog/duolingo-s-ai-powered-video-call-brings-lily-to-life)). Eight head and eight body idle moves mix into 64 variations. She leans in to listen and tilts her head when an answer is slow, so "it looks like she's pondering."
- **Siri** ([Apple newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/06/introducing-apple-intelligence-for-iphone-ipad-and-mac/)). "An elegant glowing light that wraps around the edge of the screen" while it is active.
- **Gemini's look** ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/gemini-ai-visual-design)). Gradients have a sharp front edge and a soft tail. Movement inside the shape means thinking; rippling rings mean voice. "When a system is hard to approach, the design must be soft."

## 2. Motion and 3D web work that makes people say "wow"
- **Igloo Inc**, Awwwards Site of the Year 2024 ([case study](https://www.awwwards.com/igloo-inc-case-study.html)). Each project sits frozen in a computer-grown ice block. Particles swirl into a new shape for each link and glow as they speed up. The UI is drawn in WebGL, with letters that scramble. Borrow: fact cards frozen in glass, and particle text.
- **Messenger** by Abeto, Developer Site of the Year 2025 ([case study](https://www.awwwards.com/messenger.html)). A tiny planet you can walk around, with a deliberately "a bit imperfect and hand-drawn" outline style. One small colour palette image changes the mood of the whole world at once. Borrow: a miniature world where the Peer walks between source "buildings".
- **Bruno Simon's 2025 portfolio** ([case study](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)). An island you drive around, with about 78,400 grass blades of one triangle each and one palette image for every colour. A toolkit for miniature worlds.
- **Lando Norris site** by OFF+BRAND, Site of the Year 2025 ([site](https://landonorris.com/)). The current bar for bold type, 3D and pacing.
- **Stripe globe** ([Stripe blog](https://stripe.com/blog/globe)). About 60,000 dots on a sunflower spiral, with curved arcs that draw themselves in. Borrow: arcs of light from each source to the Peer.
- **Software Galaxies** ([anvaka](https://anvaka.github.io/pm/)). 3D star maps of code libraries that you fly through. Borrow: "What I learned" as a constellation.
- **3d-force-graph** ([GitHub](https://github.com/vasturiano/3d-force-graph)). A three.js graph with custom node shapes, particles running along the links, and a glow example. The quickest route to a knowledge graph.
- **Liquid Glass** ([Apple newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-introduces-a-delightful-and-elegant-new-software-design/), [Apple's materials guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). Glass is for controls only: "Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer." Clear glass over bright pictures may need a 35% dark layer behind it. visionOS glass gets brighter or darker with the room around it.
- **Linear's own glass** ([Linear blog](https://linear.app/blog/linear-liquid-glass)). Blur, plus a gradient, plus a shine lit by a light that moves as you scroll. They left out bending of the background because it hurt reading.
- **Glass that bends light in the browser** ([kube.io](https://kube.io/blog/liquid-glass-css-svg)). Built from an SVG filter plus physics. It works only in Chrome-family browsers, which is fine for headless Chrome.
- **Edge DevTools 3D View** ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/microsoft-edge/devtools/3d-view)). A web page pulled apart into stacked layers, with a slider that peels them away one by one. Borrow: an exploded shot of the Peer's page.
- **Folding the DOM** ([Josh Comeau](https://joshwcomeau.com/react/folding-the-dom/)). Folds a real page element like paper.
- **Paper and ink kit:**
  - [StPageFlip](https://github.com/Nodlik/StPageFlip): page turns with shadows.
  - [Tegaki](https://github.com/gkurt/tegaki): any font written out stroke by stroke.
  - [Vara.js](https://github.com/akzhy/Vara): handwriting in SVG.
  - [Rough Notation](https://github.com/rough-stuff/rough-notation): hand-drawn underlines, circles and highlights, played in order.
- **Particles that dissolve** ([Codrops: dissolve](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/02/17/implementing-a-dissolve-effect-with-shaders-and-particles-in-three-js/); [Codrops: text into dust](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/01/28/webgpu-gommage-effect-dissolving-msdf-text-into-dust-and-petals-with-three-js-tsl/)). An object breaks up, particles fly off its edge, and the edge glows. The second does the same to text.

## 3. Motion patterns for AI states and trust
**States**
- **Echo light ring** ([Amazon guidelines](https://developer.amazon.com/en-US/alexa/branding/echo-guidelines/identity-guidelines/light-ring)). Listening is blue pointing toward you, Thinking is alternating blue, Responding is blue pointing again. Mic off is solid red, and every other state has its own colour and rhythm. Borrow: one colour for the Peer, rhythm to show its state, and one colour kept only for "off or private".
- **Suggested mapping, built from these sources:**
  - Idle: slow, varied breathing (like Lily).
  - Listening: the light leans toward the input (like the Echo).
  - Thinking: a swirl inside the shape (like Gemini).
  - Working: light runs along the arcs, and each source chip glows as it is read (like Chrome's underline).
  - Done: the energy gathers to a point, then lets go (Gemini's "anticipation, then release").

**Trust**
- **Carbon for AI** ([IBM](https://carbondesignsystem.com/guidelines/carbon-for-ai/)). "The AI label is the primary indicator." Every AI part carries the label and a pop-up that explains it. Glow is "strictly intended to identify" AI. Once a person edits AI text it drops the AI style, and a "revert to AI" button brings it back. Borrow: only the Peer ever glows.
- **Shape of AI** ([pattern library](https://www.shapeof.ai/)). Ready names for what each screen needs, for example:
  - Action plan, Stream of Thought, Citations, Memory, Controls.
  - Disclosure, Consent, Data ownership, Footprints, Watermark.
  - Avatar, Color, Name, Personality.
- **"As you", read-only.** [Microsoft's Copilot privacy page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/microsoft-365-copilot-privacy) says it "only surfaces organizational data to which individual users have at least view permissions." Glean says citations "never grant new access." Borrow: a "seen as you · view only" stamp on each source, using an eye icon and never a pencil.
- **Nothing kept on the server** ([Apple Private Cloud Compute](https://security.apple.com/blog/private-cloud-compute/)). Data is used only for the request and not kept afterwards. Borrow: data that visibly dissolves after use.

## 4. Rendering frame by frame in headless Chrome
**Rules**
- **Every frame must depend only on the frame number.** [Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/troubleshooting/css-animations) explains that CSS animations and transitions flicker or go blank, because they don't know which frame is being drawn. Frames are drawn [in parallel](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/using-randomness), so `Math.random()` breaks; use seeded randomness instead.
- **If you don't use Remotion, pause everything and jump to the right time:**
  - set [`Animation.currentTime`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Animation/currentTime) on every `document.getAnimations()`;
  - call GSAP's [`seek()`](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/Timeline/seek()) on a paused timeline;
  - set Theatre.js [`sheet.sequence.position = t`](https://www.theatrejs.com/docs/latest/api/core).
- **Libraries with their own timers.** [timecut](https://github.com/tungs/timecut) swaps the page's clock (`Date.now`, `performance.now`, `requestAnimationFrame`, `setTimeout`, `setInterval`) for a fake one, but it can't control CSS animations. StPageFlip, Tegaki and Rough Notation need this, or a home-made version driven by time.
- **Exact frames.** Chrome's [`HeadlessExperimental.beginFrame`](https://chromedevtools.github.io/devtools-protocol/tot/HeadlessExperimental/) draws one frame on demand. It runs in chrome-headless-shell with `--run-all-compositor-stages-before-draw`. Chromium engineers warned it wasn't fully repeatable in every case, so test it.
- **GPU.** three.js needs [`--gl=angle`](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/three). [Remotion's GPU notes](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/gl-options) warn that `angle` leaks memory, so long renders should be split into parts.
- **Fonts.** Wait for [`document.fonts.ready`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/FontFaceSet/ready) before frame 0.

**Effects**
- **Glow.** Use [selective bloom](https://react-postprocessing.docs.pmnd.rs/effects/bloom): `luminanceThreshold` 1, and only materials with emissive above 1 and `toneMapped={false}` glow. Each frame stands alone, so it is safe.
- **Soft shadows.** [ContactShadows](https://drei.docs.pmnd.rs/staging/contact-shadows) with `frames={1}` for still scenes. [AccumulativeShadows](https://drei.docs.pmnd.rs/staging/accumulative-shadows) builds up over many frames, which breaks jump-to-frame renders: let it finish building before frame 0, or bake the shadows.
- **Depth of field.** [DepthOfField](https://react-postprocessing.docs.pmnd.rs/effects/depth-of-field), with `target` moved over time for a focus pull.
- **Glass.** [MeshTransmissionMaterial](https://drei.docs.pmnd.rs/shaders/mesh-transmission-material), with `temporalDistortion` kept at 0. CSS `backdrop-filter` is the riskiest effect:
  - combined with transforms it can capture half-drawn tiles ([FrameScript](https://frame-script.github.io/FrameScript/known-issue));
  - it looks different without a GPU ([Playwright #23559](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/issues/23559)).

  Prefer WebGL glass, or a pre-blurred copy of the background.
- **Particles and graphs.** Compute positions from a seed and the time directly; don't advance them step by step each frame. For 3d-force-graph, run `warmupTicks`, then freeze it with `cooldownTicks(0)`, or save the positions to a file.
- **Handwriting.** Drive the SVG `stroke-dashoffset` from time. Give Rough.js a [`seed`](https://github.com/rough-stuff/rough/wiki) so sketchy lines don't wobble between frames.

## Top 10, ranked
1. **Remotion's render rules** – the base for every concept.
2. **Google Design on Gemini** – motion that means something in each AI state.
3. **Carbon for AI** – the always-visible AI label, and glow kept only for AI.
4. **Shape of AI** – a name for every trust pattern and every "AI at work" pattern.
5. **Igloo Inc case study** – particle text, frozen cards, UI drawn in WebGL.
6. **Messenger case study** – a miniature world, a hand-drawn look, one colour palette.
7. **Glean citations docs** – hover previews, and "never grant new access".
8. **Granola launch post** – two inks, and zoom to the quote.
9. **Apple's materials guidelines** – how to make glass that stays readable.
10. **Echo light ring** – a complete, tested set of states.

## TLDR
- The strongest ideas are shapes of light that show the Peer's state (Gemini, the Echo light ring) and glow kept only for the AI (IBM Carbon).
- For trust, put a source-and-date chip on every fact, use one ink for the person and another for the AI (Granola), and stamp each source "seen as you, view only".
- For wow: particle text and frozen cards (Igloo), a tiny hand-drawn world (Messenger), and glass that follows Apple's rules.
- For rendering: tie every animation to the frame number. Avoid CSS animations, unseeded randomness and CSS `backdrop-filter`, and use WebGL glass and glow instead.
