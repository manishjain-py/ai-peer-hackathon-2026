## What the record says

### 1. A lifelike copy of a real person is the "creepy" zone

- Reid Hoffman's deepfake twin, "Reid AI" (2024): Brian Solis's headline calls it "Both Creepy
  and Mind-Blowing" **[checked]**
  ([briansolis.com](https://briansolis.com/2024/05/ainsights-reid-hoffman-interviews-his-ai-digital-twin-and-its-both-creepy-and-mind-blowing/)).
- Zoom's CEO opened an earnings call with his AI avatar (22 May 2025): "I am proud to be among
  the first CEOs to use an avatar in an earnings call" **[checked]**
  ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/22/after-klarna-zooms-ceo-also-uses-an-ai-avatar-on-quarterly-call/)).
  Klarna's CEO did the same days earlier **[search only]**.
- Zoom's photo-real meeting avatars (17 Sep 2025): TechCrunch notes "deepfake risks involved with
  the misuse of personas" **[checked]**
  ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/17/zoom-launches-a-cross-application-ai-notetaker-ai-avatars-and-more-in-its-latest-update)).
- Zoom's founder in 2024: he would send "a digital version of myself" to meetings; coverage was
  mostly mocking **[search only]**.

### 2. The big brands keep the assistant non-human, and inside the device

- Microsoft's Copilot character Mico (23 Oct 2025): "a floating cartoon face shaped like a blob or
  flame". Microsoft saw some firms avoid giving AI any embodiment and others build AI
  girlfriends; its designer: "Those two paths don't really resonate with us that much"
  **[checked]** ([AP via News4JAX](https://www.news4jax.com/business/2025/10/23/microsoft-hopes-mico-succeeds-where-clippy-failed-as-tech-companies-warily-imbue-ai-with-personality/)).
  A character, but not a human: the middle path. Some press called it "a blob with eyes"
  **[search only]**.
- Apple's Siri (2024): "an elegant glowing light that wraps around the edge of the screen when
  Siri is active" **[checked]**
  ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/06/introducing-apple-intelligence-for-iphone-ipad-and-mac/)).
- Google's Gemini: soft round shapes and gradients, motion with a clear start and end **[read]**
  ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/gemini-ai-visual-design)). ChatGPT's voice mode
  is a pulsing ball **[search only]**.
- No product film was found in which an AI steps out of a device and goes back into it; the idea
  comes from films (Baymax in *Big Hero 6*, a genie and his lamp, R2-D2's hologram) **[search
  only / memory]**. So our entrance would be new for this kind of film.

### 3. In meetings and in Slack: a named non-person, shown openly

- Zoom's AI Companion: "an illuminated AI Sparkle icon in the top-right corner of the meeting
  window", and a notice above the chat box "AI agents present" **[checked]**
  ([Zoom whitepaper](https://library.zoom.com/ai/ai-whitepaper/user-transparency-and-notice)).
- Meeting bots join as a separate, named participant: "It appears as Fireflies.ai Notetaker"
  **[checked]** ([Fireflies FAQ](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/9554534786-how-fireflies-joins-and-records-your-meetings-faqs)).
  Otter's is named after its owner ("Ina's OtterPilot") **[search only: the page is behind a bot
  check]**. "Ram's AI Peer" follows this pattern.
- The backlash against meeting bots (the Otter lawsuit, university bans, "Please Stop Inviting AI
  Notetakers to Meetings") is about consent and silent recording, not about how the bot looks
  **[search only]**: so the Peer should announce itself in the Zoom scene, as the story already has it.
- Slack's own design guide for agents: "Human-first names can blur the line between person and
  agent"; the avatar "should be clearly non-human, and match its purpose"; actions taken with
  someone's identity are labelled "on behalf of" that person **[checked]**
  ([Slack, agent design](https://docs.slack.dev/ai/agent-design/)). It also asks for sources
  with links and a quick "Working on it..." status **[read]**.
- Slack's help centre: "Because agents use AI, they can be wrong... Use human judgement when
  appropriate." **[read]** ([Slack help](https://slack.com/help/articles/33076000248851)).

### 4. Mascots

- Clippy failed because it interrupted unasked and did not leave people "in control" **[read]**
  ([Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2005/4/28/what-not-to-do-with-a/)).
- An owl as a product mascot is Duolingo's (Duo); fireflies are a meeting-bot brand
  (Fireflies.ai) **[the helper's advice; Duo is common knowledge]**. A white owl that brings
  messages also recalls Harry Potter's owl post (ai-peer-video-wp2-4's own note).

## What it means for the Peer (ai-peer-video-wp2-4's reading)

1. Manish's instinct is backed by the record: a lifelike copy of a person reads as creepy or as a
   deepfake. A non-human character is the path the big brands chose.
2. Our label is already the accepted one: a named non-person, "Ram's AI Peer", like
   "Fireflies.ai Notetaker". Its picture should be a designed, clearly non-human face, not
   initials and not Ram's face (Slack's guide).
3. The Peer's own mark (the mint rounded square with two arches and two dots) is already its face
   on the hook's cards and the tag. A character made from it gives "one face everywhere" for free.
4. Coming out of a device and going back into it is new for this kind of film, and it is what
   makes the Peer feel like technology rather than a spirit: the light must plainly start at
   the device and end there.
5. For the Zoom and Slack screens (Phase B): the Peer joins as a separate, named tile with its
   face, an AI notice is visible to everyone, it says it is an AI; in Slack its avatar is
   non-human, its reply cites sources with links, and it says it acts "on behalf of" Ram.
