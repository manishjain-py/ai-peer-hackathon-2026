### Inferences
- The two arenas disagree most on Gemini Omni Flash 1.2: Arena puts it first on both boards, AA puts it #5 (text-to-video) and #14 (image-to-video). I trust AA more for this film: it tests 10-second clips at up to 1080p with vetted voters and gives category scores (lip-sync, anatomy, VFX edits), while Arena's 1.2 rows are marked "Preliminary" with under 2,700 votes and its Seedance rows are 720p runs. Both agree on the bigger picture: the same four families lead, and Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0, Runway and Luma trail.
- The leaders are statistically close. On AA text-to-video with audio, ranks 1 to 8 span 1175 to 1150 with intervals of ±13 to ±15, so rank order inside the top group is noise. Capability, price and content rules should decide, not rank.
- Much of the AA top 10 is one base model under several names: Utopai X, HeyGen Video 1, MiniMax H3 Max, P-Video-2-Pro, Hyperflow H3 and Pika H3 Flex are all "derived" from MiniMax H3. So "MiniMax H3 family" is one real choice with several hosts and price points.
- Kling 4.0 is the main unknown: it is a month old, absent from both arenas, and its predecessor (Kling 3.0) is the 1000-point anchor far below the leaders. Its hands-on results (section 2) suggest it belongs in the top group for multi-shot control and character consistency, but that is not yet proven by a blind arena.

## 2. What can each leading model do (length, resolution, audio, references, multi-shot, camera, editing, speed)?

### Takeaway
The leaders now share one shape: 15 to 30 second clips, 1080p (some 2K or 4K), native audio with lip-synced dialogue, first-frame and last-frame control, many reference inputs, multi-shot prompting and a built-in edit mode. The real differences are reference limits and rules (Seedance takes 30 images but no real faces; Kling 4.0 takes 7 subjects with voices; Gemini Omni takes 6 images for 2 characters), clip length (30 s for Seedance 2.5, Wan 3.0 and Kling 4.0; 15 s for Gemini Omni and MiniMax H3) and price (from $0.02 to $1.16 per second).

### Inferences
- For our five shot types the features that matter most are: (a) a strict reference system for the person and the double, (b) a first-plus-last-frame or keyframe mode for the VFX beat and the morphs, (c) lip-synced English dialogue, (d) an edit mode to fix one thing without a re-roll. Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash, Seedance 2.5, Kling 4.0, Wan 3.0 and MiniMax H3 each have all four; Veo 3.1, Runway Gen-4.5 and Luma Ray 3.2 each miss at least one (length, audio or references).

## 3. Which models and which technique can do the hero VFX of shot 1 (a translucent glowing double rising out of a sleeping body)?

### Takeaway

The strongest hard evidence is for the edit route: on AA's "visual-effects-editing" and "complex-edits" categories Gemini Omni Flash 1.2 is first and Wan 3.0 second, and keyframe tests show Seedance 2.5 and Kling 4.0 both land a given first and last frame.

### Inferences
- Ranked techniques for shot 1, best first (my judgement from the evidence above; none was tested here):
  1. **Keyframes plus stills.** Make three or four stills in an image model first (sleeper in bed; double half-risen and translucent; double standing by the bed; double seated at the laptop), then let the video model move between them: Kling 4.0 (several keyframes, up to 30 s, so the rise, stand and walk can be one take) or Seedance 2.5 or Gemini Omni (first plus last frame, done as two or three clips). Reason: the hard part, the look of the translucent glowing double and the fact that it is the same person, is fixed in stills where it can be judged and retried cheaply; the video model only has to animate between known frames, which the CellCog test shows both Seedance and Kling do reliably.
  2. **Composite two plates.** Generate (or film) a locked-off plate of the sleeper, and a second clip from the same first frame where the person sits up, stands and walks to the desk. Cut the moving figure out (LTX-2.5 alpha-gen, or generate it on a plain background) and lay it over the sleeper plate in the editor with reduced opacity and a glow. Reason: the double is translucent "by construction", the two bodies can never merge, and the effect can be tuned frame by frame. This is the safest fallback and the best choice if the keyframe route smears the moment of separation.
  3. **Video-to-video edit.** Take a clean base clip of the person asleep and ask Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash or Wan 3.0 to add the rising double (the two best models on AA's VFX-edit category). Good for the 5 to 10 second separation beat; weaker for the long walk to the desk, and Gemini caps edit inputs at 15 s. Seedance 2.5 is a poor fit here if the base clip shows a real person.
  4. **Single prompt.** Use only for cheap look tests. It works for a generic "soul rises and floats" (the template sites prove that), but our shot also needs the double to match the sleeper, stand, walk and sit, and single prompts tend to merge or duplicate bodies.
- A locked camera helps every route: both keyframes and plates line up, and the Morphic examples are almost all wide, static shots.
- Starting prompt for the separation beat (untested, built from the Morphic tips): "Wide locked-off shot, night bedroom, cool moonlight. A man lies asleep in bed and does not move. A translucent blue-white glowing copy of the same man slowly sits up out of his body, stands beside the bed and looks down at the sleeper. The sleeper stays still under the blanket. Only two figures: one solid and asleep, one glowing and see-through. Slow, deliberate motion. One continuous shot, no cuts, no music, no subtitles."
- For the stylized variant the choice of model changes less than the choice of look: lock the 3D-animation look in the stills (one image model, one style reference) and animate with Seedance 2.5 or Wan 3.0, which lead the animation categories.

### Gaps
- No creator test, forum thread or vendor example was found that shows "astral projection" or "soul leaving body" on Gemini Omni 1.2, Seedance 2.5, Kling 4.0, Wan 3.0 or MiniMax H3, so the ranking above is reasoned, not observed. The searches returned template pages and prompt galleries only.
- No test was found of "double exposure" style prompts on the current models.
- Whether Gemini Omni's or Wan 3.0's edit mode renders true see-through figures (background visible through the body) is unknown; a paid test of two or three clips would settle it.
- Whether a glowing AI-made human face trips Seedance's real-face filter is unknown (see section 6).

## 5. Which web-only subscriptions give "unlimited" or relaxed generation, and what are their watermark rules?

### Takeaway
None of the plans I could read gives unlimited generation on a lead model: Google Flow and Runway are credit-based (Runway's top plan is now "Max" with 9,500 credits, not an unlimited mode). ## 7. What are the known weaknesses today, and the standard fixes?

### Takeaway
Lip-sync itself is close to solved on the leaders; what still fails is the detail around it: a misread or clipped word, a voice that changes between lines, timing that drifts in long takes, garbled background text, hands on props, and identity that changes after a cut or an occlusion. The standard fixes are to draft at low resolution, pin identity with stills and references, keep one speaker per shot with timed lines, change one thing per retry, and repair single lines with a lip-sync or edit pass instead of re-rolling.

### Cited Findings
- Wrong or drifting dialogue: in a 30-second test Seedance 2.5 misread a word and Kling 4.0's voices changed between lines even though mouths were in sync; the article's own lesson was that lip-sync is "solved" but line accuracy is not. - [Atlas Cloud test, 26 Oct 2026](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/kling-4-vs-seedance-2-5-vs-wan-3-halloween-fight-test)
- Dialogue failure list for Seedance 2.5: mouth not matching words; voice changing between shots (fix: reference audio); last word clipped (fix: give the line about one extra second); subtitles drawn on screen (fix: "no subtitles"); music under dialogue (fix: "no music"); two speakers overlapping (fix: one speaker per shot); wording and timing vary per take, "so check every take". - [WaveSpeed guide, 25 Oct 2026](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/ai-guides/ai-lip-sync-dialogue-scenes-with-seedance-2-5/)
- Fixing one bad line without a re-roll: (1) regenerate only that line by splitting the shot; (2) re-sync the approved clip with a lip-sync model and corrected audio, "usually cheapest"; (3) rebuild the close-up from a sharp front-facing frame with a talking-avatar model and the recorded line. - [WaveSpeed guide](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/ai-guides/ai-lip-sync-dialogue-scenes-with-seedance-2-5/)
- Supplied-audio lip-sync is not word-exact when done inside the video model: similarity to the supplied track was 0.54 (Seedance 2.5) and 0.59 (Kling 4.0) where 1.00 is an exact copy. - [CellCog test, 29 Oct 2026](https://cellcog.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-vs-kling-4-0/)
- Slow-motion and filler: "Give it one small movement and the extra time usually turns into waiting, repetition, or slow motion." Fix: fill the clip with timed beats and add constraint lines such as "No cuts, no slow motion". - [fal Seedance 2.5 prompting guide, 7 Aug 2026](https://fal.ai/learn/devs/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- Identity drift: a subject hidden behind an object "can return changed" (fix: restate identity, clothing, props, direction and speed); multiple references "bleed into each other" (fix: give each reference "one narrow job" and say what not to copy from it); continue a scene from the real last frame of the previous clip. - [fal Seedance 2.5 prompting guide](https://fal.ai/learn/devs/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide)
- References beat text in Kling 4.0: it kept sunset lighting from the reference images although the prompt asked for a night storm, which cost it on atmosphere (6.0 vs 9.0 for Seedance). Fix: make the reference stills already match the scene's time of day and mood. - [Atlas Cloud test](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/kling-4-vs-seedance-2-5-vs-wan-3-halloween-fight-test)
- Timing in long takes: over 30 seconds Kling 4.0 hit all four timed beats slightly early and produced about 14 cuts against an 11-shot brief; Seedance 2.5 dropped two scripted beats; Wan 3.0 hit middle beats late and moved the ending. - [SandBase test, 23 Oct 2026](https://blog.sandbase.ai/kling-4-0-vs-seedance-2-5-veo-3-1-2026/); [Atlas Cloud test](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/kling-4-vs-seedance-2-5-vs-wan-3-halloween-fight-test)
- Hands, props and physics: Kling 4.0's hand "warped on a cup"; Seedance 2.5's pancake "hung in the air for about 1.5 seconds"; Seedance showed heavy motion blur on arms; Kling 4.0 added two unexplained shadows. - [CellCog test](https://cellcog.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-vs-kling-4-0/); [SandBase test](https://blog.sandbase.ai/kling-4-0-vs-seedance-2-5-veo-3-1-2026/)
- On-screen text: short quoted signs can be exact (both Seedance 2.5 and Kling 4.0 spelled "OPEN LATE" and "OAT LATTE $5" correctly), but unscripted background signs came out as "pseudo-Chinese nonsense" in both 30-second clips; Alibaba says Wan 3.0's "on-screen text rendering accuracy" is still being refined. Fix: quote the exact words, their position and timing. - [CellCog test](https://cellcog.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-vs-kling-4-0/); [SandBase test](https://blog.sandbase.ai/kling-4-0-vs-seedance-2-5-veo-3-1-2026/); [Alibaba blog](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/wan3-0-30-second-ai-video-generation-from-any-input_603452); [fal Kling 4.0 how-to](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/how-to-use-kling-4-0)
- Unwanted cuts: Gemini Omni "may create several shots" by default (fix: ask for "a single continuous shot" or "no scene cuts"); Kling 4.0 cut to a wider shot despite a static-camera prompt. - [Omni docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/omni); [CellCog test](https://cellcog.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-vs-kling-4-0/)
- Edits that change too much: extra clauses in a Gemini Omni edit prompt caused unrequested changes (a jacket edit also altered a sign and added two cars). Fix: one short sentence, one change per turn, end with "Keep everything else the same", branch from the last good result. - [fal Gemini Omni edit guide, 20 Jul 2026](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/how-to-edit-videos-with-gemini-omni-flash)
- Maker-stated limits: Google says work continues on "fast sports action, rapid script changes and complex physics" and that non-English speech is not evaluated; multi-video reference prompting "may result in degraded model performance". - [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/introducing-gemini-omni-1-2-flash/); [Omni docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/omni)
- Soft or low-resolution finals: Gemini Omni's 1080p and 4K are upscales of a 720p generation; MiniMax H3 reaches 2K by "regeneration" of its 768P result ($0.05/s); FLUX 3 has `draft-enhance`; Seedance 2.5 completes a 480p draft at 1080p; separate upscalers exist (`blackforestlabs/flux-video-upscale`, `lightricks/ltx-2.5/upscale` to 8K, Topaz endpoints, Runway's `magnific_video_upscaler_creative`) and frame-rate tools (`topaz/interpolate/video`, Runway `enhance_frame_rate`). - [Gemini API pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing); [MiniMax video pricing](https://platform.minimax.io/docs/guides/pricing-video); [fal catalog](https://fal.ai/api/models?keywords=upscale); [Runway API pricing](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/guides/pricing/)
- Take-to-take variance: one-off comparisons are unreliable because the same prompt on the same model gives different results on different runs (Higgsfield blog, July 2026, search snippet); the SandBase author says a single clip per model "cannot rank models". - [Higgsfield blog](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/most-reliable-ai-video-generators-2026); [SandBase test](https://blog.sandbase.ai/kling-4-0-vs-seedance-2-5-veo-3-1-2026/)

### Inferences
- For the film's two dialogue shots the safest design is: record or synthesise the exact line as audio first, generate the shot with the line written in the prompt, and if the take is good but a word is wrong, repair it with a supplied-audio lip-sync pass (`minimax/h3-max/lip-sync/image-to-video`, `fal-ai/sync-lipsync/v3/image-to-video` or `veed/lipsync/v2`) instead of re-rolling. This removes misread words and voice drift, the two failures seen in tests.
- Keep every dialogue shot short (4 to 8 seconds, one speaker) and cut between speakers in the edit; the models' own multi-speaker scenes are the weakest case in every guide read.
- Plan on three to eight takes per shot and judge each take by eye; no model in any test delivered every scripted beat on the first try.
- 720p softness is now mostly a workflow choice, not a model limit: draft at 360p to 480p, finish at 1080p, and upscale only the chosen takes.

### Gaps
- No dated source was found that measures flicker or face drift across cuts on the current models; the points above come from four hands-on tests and two prompting guides.
- No independent hands-on test of Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash (released nine days ago) was found; its weaknesses here are Google's own statements and a guide written for the first version.
- Most hands-on tests found were written by hubs or resellers (Atlas Cloud, SandBase, CellCog, WaveSpeed, fal). Their measurements look careful, but each has something to sell; no test by an unaffiliated working filmmaker dated September to November 2026 was found before the search budget ran out.
