# Likeness, consistent character, talking shots, performance transfer and voice for one consenting real person (tool state on 14 November 2026)

How to read these notes: every page was read on 14 November 2026. "Primary" means the vendor's own docs, help centre, pricing page or blog. "Third-party" means a blog, reseller or aggregator and is a lead, not proof. "Search summary" means I could not open the page itself (usually HTTP 403) and the fact comes from the search tool's summary of that page. Prices are US dollars. Almost every hands-on test found is a single run per model, so treat rankings as tendencies. No test found used a South Asian subject.

## 1. Reference-based likeness in video: which models accept a real person's photos, which block them, and what consent step each needs

### Takeaway
In November 2026 the tools split into three groups: (1) verified "this is me" avatars (Google's avatar in Flow, Gemini and Vids; HeyGen's digital twin), which are the sanctioned way to put your own face in; (2) open reference models that accepted a creator's own face in recent tests with no verification step (Kling 4.0; Wan 3 and MiniMax H3 state no rule on their fal pages); (3) models that block real faces at input unless you pass a verification path (Seedance 2.0 and 2.5; Google's developer API for Veo and Gemini Omni). Sora, including its cameo feature, is gone. Likeness from a few reference photos still drifts across shots, and no vendor publishes a number for it.

### Inferences
- For a consenting creator using their own face, the lowest-friction photoreal video routes today are Kling 4.0 (references accepted in tests, no stated block) and Google's `@me` avatar (verified, built for exactly this). Seedance 2.5 is the strongest identity-holder in the one fictional-character test but is the hardest to feed a real face.
- Google's API and its consumer apps behave differently: the API blocks "recognizable people" and has blocked a company's own footage, while the consumer avatar is the approved path. A script-driven pipeline should not plan on Veo or Omni API calls with real face photos.
- Since Seedance's filter also rejects AI-generated photoreal faces, keyframes of the creator made in an image model are likely to be rejected there too unless the verified path is used.
- "How many photos" in practice: 3 (Veo), 3 per character (Omni), 4 or a short clip (Kling 3.0 element), up to 10 images plus videos (Kling 4.0, Wan 3), 20 to 80 (Soul ID). More angles reduce guessing; none of the sources quantify stability across lighting.

### Gaps
- No official Google page announcing a "Veo 4" was found; third-party pages in search results conflict on whether it exists, so Veo 3.1 and Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash are treated as Google's current video models here.
- A single blog in search results says Grok Imagine now blocks most prompts anchored to a real identifiable person; not opened, not verified.
- No official Kling statement on real-face references or any verification flow was found; evidence is two hands-on tests and one unverified reseller claim.
- No official ByteDance page describing the Seedance 2.5 consumer (Dreamina) verification steps was found; whether an individual outside China can complete it is unknown.
- Whether the Google avatar is offered in India specifically, and how it handles non-American English accents, was not stated on the help pages.
- No independent multi-run benchmark of real-person likeness across video models exists in what I found; all tests are single runs with fictional or AI-made subjects, except two creator tests.
- Luma, Vidu and PixVerse reference features were not checked.

## 2. Image models for a keyframe-first workflow, and whether a face LoRA still beats reference images

### Takeaway
For new scenes, outfits and lighting from a real person's photos, Google's Nano Banana line (Nano Banana Pro and Nano Banana 2.1) is the most consistently named identity-keeper, with Seedream 5.0 Pro and FLUX 3 close behind in one October 2026 test and GPT Image 2.5 drifting more on faces. No controlled test shows whether a LoRA beats references for one real face in late 2026; vendor guidance is to use references for a handful of images and train a LoRA (about $2 to $6 on fal, 10 or more photos) when the same face must hold across hundreds of images or when references start to drift.

### Inferences
- A sensible default is: build the character sheet and scene keyframes with Nano Banana Pro or 2.1 from 5 to 14 real photos, always editing from the original photos rather than from earlier outputs, because iterative edits are where identity degrades.
- A LoRA is not needed for a short film with a few dozen keyframes; it becomes worth $4 to $6 if reference-based keyframes drift at unusual angles, or if the stylized look needs to be locked.
- The benchmark paper appears to test its authors' own identity system alongside the others, so it may not be independent; its general finding matches the hands-on tests.

### Gaps
- No controlled head-to-head of "LoRA versus reference images" for one real face in a late-2026 model was found.
- Training time for FLUX LoRAs on fal is not stated on the pages read.
- No official statement from OpenAI or ByteDance on editing photos of yourself in GPT Image 2.x or Seedream 5 was found. Unverified third-party tutorials in search results say GPT Image 2 sometimes refuses or alters identifiable real faces, including when called through fal.
- The Nano Banana 2.1 release date (one page says general availability on 6 Oct 2026) was not confirmed on a Google page.
- No image test used a South Asian subject.

## 3. Stylized version: a consistent 3D-animated or illustrated character that still reads as the person

### Takeaway
The common method is two steps: make a stylized character sheet of the person in an image model, then use it as the reference or first frame for video. Stylized characters meet less friction than photoreal faces on the strict video models, but no test found measures how well a 3D-animated character keeps a specific real person's likeness, so this needs the creator's own eye.

## 5. Performance transfer and character replacement

### Takeaway
Runway Act-Two is the best-documented way to film the real person on a phone and move that acting onto a generated character (5 credits per second, clips up to 30 seconds). Wan Animate, Kling Motion Control and MiniMax H3 Max "recast" do the same or swap a person inside existing footage; face drift and hands are the common weak points, and Google's API has blocked edits of footage containing a real person.

### Inferences
- This route removes the identity problem entirely: both figures are the real person, so no model can drift the face or skin tone. It also needs no real-face permission from any video model.

- Sending real footage of the creator to fal or Beeble is the same kind of upload as any cloud render; none of the pages read list a face-policy block for segmentation or matting.

## 7. Voice: cloning, expressive speech, multi-speaker dialogue, consent, price

### Takeaway
ElevenLabs remains the reference choice, and it changed on 28 September 2026: Eleven v4 replaced v3 as the flagship, supports professional clones (v3 did not), and instant clones now work from about 10 seconds of audio. An instant clone needs only a rights confirmation and the $6 plan; a professional clone needs 30 minutes to 3 hours of audio, the $22 plan, and a read-aloud verification that the voice is yours. Cartesia is the main rival for cloning by API; OpenAI's recording-based custom voices are gated to approved customers.

### Cited Findings
- Eleven v4 and v4 Turbo launched 28 Sep 2026. Professional clones "weren't supported in v3" and are supported in v4; instant clones "can now capture voices with high fidelity using just 10 seconds of audio"; `eleven_v4` works with the Text to Dialogue API for multi-speaker content. Source (primary, search summary of changelog and blog): [ElevenLabs changelog](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/changelog/2026/9/28); [ElevenLabs v4 blog](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/eleven-v4)

- For this film the quickest good result is an ElevenLabs instant clone on v4 (1 to 2 minutes of clean recording, $6 plan). If the clone flattens the creator's accent, the two documented levers are a professional clone (same-language training, verified) and Cartesia's longer 60-second sample "to better retain an accent".
- v4's cross-language behaviour (dropping the original accent) matters only if lines are generated in a language other than the one recorded.
- Two characters (the person and the AI double) can be produced in one pass with Text to Dialogue using the clone twice with different delivery tags, or the double can use a lightly altered version of the clone.
- Recording the real lines for the real person and cloning only the double's lines avoids the clone having to pass as the real voice side by side.

### Gaps
- No independent test of clone accuracy for Indian or other South Asian English accents was found.
- ElevenLabs' direct per-character API price for v4 and the maximum speakers in Text to Dialogue were not on the pages read.
- Artificial Analysis leaderboards were found but not read.
- Hume's current status is unclear: one page in search results (not opened) says Hume's site no longer lists Octave text-to-speech as of November 2026.

## 8. Failure modes and known fixes, including South Asian faces

### Takeaway
The documented failures are identity drift between shots and at close-up or unusual angles, a smoothed or "same type of person" face, wrong-speaker or drifting lip-sync, altered voices, stiff bodies and bad hands. The documented fixes are procedural: short single-action shots, the same multi-angle references every time, identity kept out of the prompt, and mouth repair as a separate pass. For South Asian faces specifically, research shows image models default to lighter skin, but no 2026 source documents the effect in video or for reference-based likeness.

### Cited Findings
- Kling's own list (24 Sep 2026): faces, hair and clothing can change between shots; continuity breaks down as clips get longer; fingers warp. Workarounds: "Reuse the same reference images and keep defining details consistent", avoid changing lighting, angle, styling and setting all at once, "Keep each shot centered on one main action", generate scene by scene and sequence in the edit. Source (primary): [Kling blog](https://kling.ai/blog/limitations-of-current-ai-video-generation-technology)
- fal's fix list (22 Sep 2026): close-ups slip while wide shots hold when the reference crop and framing do not match (include head-and-shoulders panels); the character becomes unrecognisable when turning if the references lack profile or rear views; drift after a prompt rewrite means identity words in the prompt are competing with the reference (remove them); small details like one earring or freckles fail first and are an early warning. Source: [fal character consistency](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/ai-character-consistency)
- Observed drift: MiniMax H3 Max face "smoother and slightly narrower" in a close-up; Wan 3.0 jaw, age impression and proportions changed between scenes (22 Sep 2026). GPT Image 2.5: narrower jaw, more polished eyes, "the same type of person, not always the exact same person" (20 Oct 2026). Source: [CreativeClaw](https://creativeclaw.co/benchmarks/character-consistency-models/); [getimg.ai](https://getimg.ai/blog/most-consistent-ai-image-model-test)
- Identity degrades most under iterative edits, small subject scale and multi-subject scenes. Source: [arXiv 2609.04151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04151)
- Lip-sync and voice: lines assigned to the wrong speaker, misplaced sync, voice consistency "still tricky for longer projects", extra hallucinated dialogue, slightly altered voice. Fix offered: generate the video with audio first, then pass it through a sync tool, and repair the voice in the edit. Source: [Curious Refuge, May 2026](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/ai-lip-sync-workflows); [AI video supertest, 24 Feb 2026](https://dantaylorwatt.substack.com/p/ai-video-supertest-e11)
- Motion transfer: face drift is the most cited complaint in Kling Motion Control; add a facial element on version 3.0 and inspect hands. For recast, rerun with a new seed and check the face at the start and end. Source: [DIYAI guide](https://diyai.io/ai-tools/video-generation/kling-motion-control/); [fal character replacement guide](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/how-to-make-a-character-replacement-video)
- Static talking avatars: on HeyGen Avatar V, flat audio produces a static avatar because prompts have the lowest priority. Source: [HeyGen blog](https://www.heygen.com/blog/photo-avatar-vs-digital-twin-vs-avatar-v-vs-instant-avatar)
- False blocks: Seedance flags AI-generated faces as real people. Source: [Fuser](https://fuser.studio/articles/kling-4-vs-seedance-2-5); [Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-404/seedance-dont-work-correctly-detects-human-face-and-is-not-human-face-is-created-with-ia-1630600)
- Skin tone: a February 2026 audit of 3,200 images from neutral prompts found both Gemini Flash 2.5 Image and GPT Image 1.5 showed a strong "default white" bias in over 96% of outputs, measured on the Monk, PERLA and Fitzpatrick scales. Source: [arXiv 2602.12133, 12 Feb 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12133)
- A 2026 workshop paper reports some generators consistently produce lighter-skinned faces, with prompt constraints only modestly reducing the effect (search summary; per-ethnicity results not seen). Source: [EACL 2026 workshop paper](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-eacl/2026.eacl-srw.69.pdf)
- A February 2026 news report on viral AI images of South and East Asian women quotes a South Asian commentator: "We're seeing white-washed imagery". Source: [news report](https://www.fijivillage.com/news/AI-images-of-women-from-around-the-world-have-gone-viral-Do-they-promote-colourism-and-cultural-beauty-standards-x458rf)
- Voice: an instant clone may struggle with "a voice with an accent" the model has seen little of. Source: [ElevenLabs voice cloning docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/voices/voice-cloning)

### Inferences
- The skin-tone studies measure text-only prompts. With real photos as references the starting point is correct, so the likely risk is gradual lightening or smoothing across edits, relights and upscales rather than a wholesale change. A practical guard is to keep one approved, colour-accurate reference frame, compare every keyframe and clip against it, never chain edits from outputs, and do the final match in the colour grade.
- Writing the person's fixed features into the character spec (skin tone, hair, beard, glasses) helps spot drift, but fal's guidance is to keep such identity words out of scene prompts when a reference is attached; the spec is for checking, not prompting.
- "Uncanny eyes and teeth" were not specifically discussed in any source read. The general remedy implied by the sources is to avoid long held close-ups from image-plus-audio models and use a real recording or a verified twin for those shots.
- Short clips, one action each, with the same references, then a separate lip-sync pass, is the pattern every vendor and tester converges on.

### Gaps
- No 2026 source documents skin-tone or facial-feature shifts for South Asian faces in video models or in reference-based image editing, and none gives a tested fix.
- No source addressed eyes and teeth artifacts directly.
- No multi-run failure-rate numbers exist for any tool in what I found.

## 9. Recommended workflow for (a) a photoreal look and (b) a stylized 3D-animation look of the same person

### Takeaway
Use three layers by risk: real footage for the real person (and, where possible, for the "double" via the two-take composite), a verified avatar or digital twin for photoreal AI talking shots, and Kling 4.0 with Nano Banana keyframes for free-form photoreal scenes. For the stylized double, build a 3D-style character sheet from the same photos, animate it with Seedance 2.5 or Kling 4.0, and drive its acting with Runway Act-Two and an ElevenLabs v4 clone.

### Cited Findings
- Verified self-likeness exists at Google (Flow `@me`, Gemini avatar: face and voice capture, 18+, paid plan, 3 to 15 second clips, SynthID) and HeyGen (15 seconds of video plus live consent, renders up to 30 minutes). Source: [Google Labs Help](https://support.google.com/labs/answer/17102997); [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16984474); [HeyGen blog](https://www.heygen.com/blog/photo-avatar-vs-digital-twin-vs-avatar-v-vs-instant-avatar)
- Kling 4.0 accepted a creator's own face reference in October 2026 and costs $0.157 to $0.235 per second; Seedance 2.5 blocks real faces unless verified and costs about three times as much at 720p and five times at 1080p on the platform tested. Source: [creator review](https://moelueker.com/blog/kling-4-review); [fal, Kling 4.0 vs 3.0](https://fal.ai/learn/devs/kling-4-0-vs-kling-3-0); [BytePlus blog](https://byteplus.com/en/blog/dreamina-seedance2-5); [Fuser](https://fuser.studio/articles/kling-4-vs-seedance-2-5)
- Nano Banana Pro and 2.1 lead the identity tests for keyframes; fal trains a LoRA for $2 to $6. Source: [image model test](https://dantaylorwatt.substack.com/p/which-ai-image-generator-should-i); [getimg.ai](https://getimg.ai/blog/most-consistent-ai-image-model-test); [fal fine-tune guide](https://fal.ai/learn/devs/how-to-fine-tune-flux-with-the-api)
- Act-Two moves a filmed performance onto a character for 5 credits per second, up to 30 seconds. Source: [Runway API pricing](https://docs.dev.runwayml.com/guides/pricing/)
- ElevenLabs v4 instant clone from 10 seconds to 2 minutes of audio on the $6 plan. Source: [ElevenLabs changelog](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/changelog/2026/9/28); [ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing)
- Segmentation, matting and depth are on fal; Beeble Cloud adds relighting passes. Source: [fal SAM 3.1 video](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/sam-3-1/video/api); [fal BiRefNet v2 video](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/birefnet/v2/video/api); [Beeble pricing](https://beeble.ai/pricing)

### Inferences

#### (a) Photoreal
1. Capture once: 15 to 20 well-lit photos (front, three-quarter, profile, full body, neutral and smiling), a 15 to 30 second locked-off video, and 2 minutes of clean voice. This single kit feeds every tool below.
2. Voice: ElevenLabs v4 instant clone; move to a professional clone or to Cartesia only if the accent is flattened.
3. Shots where the double talks to camera: first choice is a verified route. Use HeyGen's digital twin when the cloned audio file must drive the lips or the shot is longer than 15 seconds; use Google's `@me` avatar for short prompted clips with scene changes.
4. Free-form shots (the double walking, in new places, with effects): make keyframes with Nano Banana Pro or 2.1 from the real photos, then animate in Kling 4.0 with the same reference set as an element, one action per shot, 5 to 10 seconds each. Keep Wan 3 or MiniMax H3 Max as cheaper fallbacks and expect more drift.
5. Lip repair: if a generated shot's mouth is off, re-sync with sync.so or VEED Fabric rather than regenerating.
6. Where the real person and the double share the frame: prefer the two-take locked-camera composite with matting on fal or Beeble and effects (glow, translucency, dissolve) on the matted take. It cannot drift and needs no model's permission.
7. Avoid planning around: Sora (gone), Seedance 2.5 with real photos (blocked unless verified), and Veo or Gemini Omni API calls with real-face inputs (blocked for some users).

#### (b) Stylized 3D animation
1. From the same photos, generate a 3D-animation-style character sheet (front, three-quarter, profile, full body, expressions, flat even lighting) with Nano Banana Pro or 2.1; try GPT Image 2.5 as a second opinion on style. Lock a written spec of the features that carry the likeness and check each panel against it.
2. If many stills are needed or the style wobbles, train a LoRA on 10 to 20 approved sheet images (FLUX 3 Image Klein or FLUX.2 on fal).
3. Animate with the sheet as reference: Seedance 2.5 first (best identity hold in the one test and friendlier to stylized characters), Kling 4.0 as the alternative with voice bound to the element.
4. Acting and dialogue: film the creator performing each line waist-up and transfer it with Runway Act-Two; or use OmniHuman 1.5 with the cloned audio for simple talking shots.
5. Same voice clone as in (a), optionally with different delivery tags so the double sounds like a variant of the person.

- Budget sense from the cited prices: keyframes cost cents each, video $0.10 to $0.50 per second, a LoRA $2 to $6, the voice plan $6, a matting pass cents per clip; a two to three minute film assembled from 5 to 10 second shots with retakes is on the order of tens of dollars per look, excluding subscriptions.

### Gaps
- Every step above should be proven with the creator's own photos before committing: nothing found tests a South Asian face, and all model tests were single runs.
- Whether Seedance accepts a stylized render of a real person, and whether Kling keeps accepting real faces, can change without notice; neither is written policy.
- HeyGen's API price is disputed across its own pages, and twin creation by API may need an Enterprise account; the web app route is the documented one.
- Availability of the Google avatar in the creator's country and plan was not confirmed.
