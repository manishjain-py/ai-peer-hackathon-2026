## 1. The quality bar: the best AI-made films and adverts of 2026 (and late 2025), how they were made, and what "AI slop" means

### Takeaway
The 2026 bar is story-led work under heavy human control: a solo-made 8-minute drama won the main festival prize by sheer iteration, and the admired adverts came from small teams that planned on boards, locked still frames first, generated many takes and finished with real editing and sound. "AI slop" now means work that reads as low-effort or cynical (details that drift, wrong physics, plastic realism, generic settings, no idea), and viewers punish it even when the brand says humans were involved.

**What "AI slop" means now**

- Trade definition: "low-effort, mass produced, AI-generated content". A survey cited there: 47% of Gen Z dislike AI-generated content. One agency view: "It's only when laziness is the driving force that brands risk losing engagement and consumer trust." Source: [Digiday, 20 November 2025](https://digiday.com/marketing/backlash-grows-against-ai-slop-but-marketers-remain-unfazed/)
- "'AI slop' isn't just a critique of visual quality; it's a rejection of perceived cynicism", and "Slop is what happens when random generation replaces intention/story." Source: [Make it Pop, 5 January 2026](https://makeitpop.beehiiv.com/p/make-it-pop-09-the-2026-ai-video-playbook-for-brands-how-to-avoid-the-ai-slop-trap)
- Typical visual tells: "warped bodies, uncanny expressions, and a synthetic look that feels rushed or unfinished". Source: [McGraw Hill blog, 13 May 2026](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/blog/2026/05/ai-slop-backfires-in-ads.html)
- Tells named for the Coca-Cola remake: "gliding" trucks, animals with "plastic" fur, a strange number plate. Tells of carelessness in general: "Details reset between shots, objects disappear", and "Audiences instantly spot physics errors and uncanny motion." Source: [Make it Pop, 5 January 2026](https://makeitpop.beehiiv.com/p/make-it-pop-09-the-2026-ai-video-playbook-for-brands-how-to-avoid-the-ai-slop-trap)
- Other tells listed by one commentator: vague settings with no real names or places, and clips that change every six to ten seconds, about where many generators start to distort. Source (search snippet, page not opened): [Digital Nova Scotia](https://digitalnovascotia.com/news/ai-content-trust-erosion-ingrid-deon/)
- NielsenIQ research (surveys, eye tracking and response-time tests) found viewers call AI-generated adverts more "annoying", "boring" and "confusing" than traditional ones, can readily tell them apart, and that "even high-quality AI-generated content fails to create lasting impressions". Source: [CO/AI summary of a Marketing Dive report, 18 December 2024](https://getcoai.com/news/nielsen-consumers-find-ai-generated-advertising-annoying-boring-and-confusing)
- A study cited by NPR found only 48% of Gen Z and millennial consumers felt positive about AI-generated adverts. Source: [NPR via Texas Public Radio, 23 June 2025](https://www.tpr.org/2025-06-23/an-ai-video-ad-is-making-a-splash-is-it-the-future-of-advertising)

**How the best makers avoid it**

- "Either fully polish with human VFX or lean intentionally into stylization. The danger zone is imperfect realism." Also: "human intent at the start, AI in the middle, human polish at the end", and "Frame-by-frame checks, continuity, and performance plausibility are non-negotiable." Source: [Make it Pop, 5 January 2026](https://makeitpop.beehiiv.com/p/make-it-pop-09-the-2026-ai-video-playbook-for-brands-how-to-avoid-the-ai-slop-trap)
- Match the medium to the message: "High-emotion moments (holidays, luxury, nostalgia) demand human warmth." McDonald's AI holiday chaos read as "nightmare fuel" rather than comedy. Source: [Make it Pop, 5 January 2026](https://makeitpop.beehiiv.com/p/make-it-pop-09-the-2026-ai-video-playbook-for-brands-how-to-avoid-the-ai-slop-trap)
- Anchor to real references (real product photos per shot), plan tone and pace on a board-o-matic before generating, and keep prompts simple because the images do the work. Source: [studio thread, 17 October 2025](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1979215033172127798)
- "Let AI help with scripting, but keep humans in charge of tone, pacing and story", and "real emotion starts with a human performance" for voice. Source: [Superside, 30 May 2025](https://www.superside.com/blog/how-we-used-ai-for-our-campaign-video)

### Inferences
- The work held up as good shares three habits: a clear idea decided before any generation, strict control of still frames, and normal film finishing (edit, grade, human voice, sound mix). The work mocked as slop tried to look photoreal and warm, at length, with no joke or idea of its own.
- AI footage is forgiven most when the content is openly absurd or stylised (the "unhinged" basketball advert, the self-parody of glitches) and least when it imitates a warm, realistic human moment. A glowing double rising from a sleeper, and a storybook school of magic, are both on the stylised side; a realistic office scene with realistic colleagues is on the risky side.
- For a hackathon audience that knows AI well, telling them how it was made is unlikely to rescue weak footage (the pulled adverts' "humans were involved" defence did not work), but a visible idea and tight craft will.
- Solo or two-person production of about 2 minutes is realistic: the documented solo jobs took 2 to 8 days for 30 seconds to a few minutes.

- Coca-Cola production numbers conflict across reports: search results (pages not opened, including Tom's Guide and Implicator) mention about 20 people or over 100 workers for one of the two films, and timelines of one or two months. Only the "five specialists, 70,000 clips, 30 days" figure was verified, and it comes from the makers' own video.
- A reported $450,000 Grand Prix at an Astana AI film festival (3 October 2026) surfaced only in a search snippet with no production details; not verified.

## 2. The standard professional workflow in late 2026, and its norms

### Takeaway
The settled workflow is: script, then storyboard or rough animatic, then still keyframes made with an image model, then image-to-video in short clips, then upscaling, edit, grade and a real sound mix. Clips are generated at 3 to 15 seconds and cut far shorter, makers expect many generations per usable shot, and consistency comes from reference sheets and locked start frames, not from long prompts.

### Cited Findings

**The steps**

- Google's own Veo guide recommends a multi-step route over one long prompt: make the start frame (and an end frame, or reference images of each character and the set) with its image model first, then pass them to the video model. Source: [Google Cloud blog, "Ultimate prompting guide for Veo 3.1", 16 October 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1)
- A documented advert pipeline: script, treatment, board-o-matic approved by the client, finely tuned stills, image-to-video with simple prompts, AI and human audio, edit against the board-o-matic while animation is still running, After Effects for hard fixes, human final mix. Source: [studio thread, 17 October 2025](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1979215033172127798)
- A documented agency pipeline: human script, rough storyboard, retouched style frames, start and end frames into three video tools, Premiere edit, 2D overlays, then colour grade, film grain, audio balance and transition fixes. "The storyboard doesn't have to look good. It just has to feel right." Source: [Superside, 30 May 2025](https://www.superside.com/blog/how-we-used-ai-for-our-campaign-video)
- Upscale twice: approved start frames before video generation, and the master file after export. "Skipping either shows" (paraphrase in source). Source: [maker's write-up, 8 December 2025](https://my-portfolio-website-elu.pages.dev/insights/making-of-mad-man-ai-commercial)

**Clip length the models give you (dated)**

- Veo 3.1: clips of 4, 6 or 8 seconds, 720p or 1080p, 16:9 or 9:16, with sound generated from the prompt. Source: [Google Cloud blog, 16 October 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1)
- Kling 3.0: any duration from 3 to 15 seconds; up to six shots in one generation, each with its own length, and the lengths must add up to the total (the official example is four shots of 3, 4, 5 and 3 seconds). Longer shots suit reflection, shorter shots suit urgency. Source: [Kling blog, 28 July 2026](https://kling.ai/blog/kling-3-15s-video-narrative-control-duration)
- State of the field in October 2026 according to one dated round-up: Wan 3.0 (up to 30 s), Seedance 2.5 (4 to 30 s, many image, video and audio references), Gemini Omni Flash (3 to 10 s, announced May 2026 and described as Google's recommended default), Kling 3.0 (15 s, native 4K), Runway Gen-4.5 (2 to 10 s, no native audio), Luma Ray3.2 (20 s, keyframes), Veo 3.1 (4, 6 or 8 s, up to 3 reference images, last-frame control). OpenAI's Sora app was discontinued on 26 April 2026 and its API on 24 September 2026. Source: [Pinggy round-up, updated 11 October 2026](https://pinggy.io/blog/best_video_generation_ai_models/)

**Shot length in the cut, and takes per shot**

- The 30-second basketball advert used 15 usable clips, chosen from 300 to 400 generations. Source: [NPR via Texas Public Radio, 23 June 2025](https://www.tpr.org/2025-06-23/an-ai-video-ad-is-making-a-splash-is-it-the-future-of-advertising)
- "Dozens of generations for some shots"; the tools were found to break after 5 to 6 seconds, so each scene was kept under that. Source: [Superside, 30 May 2025](https://www.superside.com/blog/how-we-used-ai-for-our-campaign-video)
- 424 image generations (mostly edits of an existing frame) for 37 or 38 video shots. Source: [Flick blog, August 2026 (vendor)](https://flick.art/blog/how-i-made-a-viking-film-with-ai)
- "Hundreds of iterations" on start frames and "dozens of attempts" on physically hard shots (a rope being cut). Source: [maker's write-up, 8 December 2025](https://my-portfolio-website-elu.pages.dev/insights/making-of-mad-man-ai-commercial)
- Google's timestamp example gives each beat 2 seconds inside one 8-second generation. Source: [Google Cloud blog, 16 October 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1)
- Talking shots: "a four-second clip holds lip-sync better than a nine-second monologue" (paraphrase in source). Source: [Flowjam, updated 20 October 2026 (vendor)](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/launch-video-scriptwriting-write-yours-in-60-minutes-2026)
- The festival Grand Prix film went the other way and held scenes for 30 seconds or more, at the price of very many generations. Source: [Let's Data Science, 19 June 2026](https://letsdatascience.com/news/runway-showcases-ai-generated-shorts-to-packed-audience-3825c8ad)

**Keeping character, wardrobe, light and lens the same**

- Character reference sheets (front, profile, three-quarter on plain grey), wardrobe sketched then turned into photoreal turnarounds, a hand sketch per shot for blocking and camera, and edits on a locked base image instead of new rolls. "A character without one drifts" (paraphrase). Source: [Flick blog, August 2026 (vendor)](https://flick.art/blog/how-i-made-a-viking-film-with-ai)
- Veo "ingredients to video": give reference images of each character, object and setting and name them by role in every prompt, for example "Using the provided images for the detective, the woman, and the office setting, create a medium shot of the detective behind his desk." Source: [Google Cloud blog, 16 October 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1)
- Kling: use reference images or video "elements" when a character, product or prop must stay recognisable, and keep the character or object description steady while the scene or camera changes. Source: [Kling prompt guide, page dated 7 August 2026](https://kling.ai/blog/kling-ai-prompt-guide)
- A custom prompt block that fixes focal length, lighting and material textures for every image, plus physical product photos attached as references; even so, models still misplaced and invented parts. Source: [maker's write-up, 8 December 2025](https://my-portfolio-website-elu.pages.dev/insights/making-of-mad-man-ai-commercial)
- First and last frame rules: the two images must match on subject identity, proportions, aspect ratio, scene layout, camera position, horizon, lighting direction and key shadows; an unrelated portrait creates a second identity target; check the face in the middle of the move, not only at the ends; split into separate shots when location, outfit and camera all change. Veo 3.1 end-frame requests are limited to 8 seconds. Source: [Magic Hour shot design guide, 2 October 2026 (vendor)](https://magichour.ai/blog/first-frame-last-frame-ai-video)

**How prompts are written for current models**

- Veo formula, in order: "[Cinematography] + [Subject] + [Action] + [Context] + [Style & Ambiance]". Dialogue goes in quotation marks; sound as "SFX: ..." and "Ambient noise: ...". Say what should be present instead of listing what to exclude. Official example: "Medium shot, a tired corporate worker, rubbing his temples in exhaustion, in front of a bulky 1980s computer in a cluttered office late at night. The scene is lit by the harsh fluorescent overhead lights and the green glow of the monochrome monitor. Retro aesthetic, shot as if on 1980s color film, slightly grainy." Source: [Google Cloud blog, 16 October 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1)
- Veo timestamp prompting for a small sequence in one clip, for example "[00:00-00:02] Medium shot from behind a young female explorer ... [00:02-00:04] Reverse shot of the explorer's freckled face ...". Source: [Google Cloud blog, 16 October 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1)
- Kling's five elements: subject, action, scene, camera, lighting or mood, in plain scene description. Name camera moves the viewer will see (push-in, pan, tilt, tracking) and only when they serve the story; use speaker labels for dialogue; for multi-shot, set the scene first and then list shots in order, for example "[Shot 1: Wide shot] ... [Shot 2: Medium shot] ... [Shot 3: Close-up shot] ...". Short pacing direction example: "A slow push-in as the character thinks." Source: [Kling prompt guide, page dated 7 August 2026](https://kling.ai/blog/kling-ai-prompt-guide)
- Sketch-to-frame prompts used in a real short: "Use the provided sketch as the only composition reference", "Camera position 100% unchanged." Performance prompt for a dialogue close-up: "Handheld camera, extreme close-up of his face. His eyes stay locked on the camera, no drift." Source: [Flick blog, August 2026 (vendor)](https://flick.art/blog/how-i-made-a-viking-film-with-ai)

**Sound and voice**

- Mixed approach in real jobs: model-generated sound and dialogue, a voice tool for extra lines, a lip-sync tool, and a human sound designer for the final mix. Source: [studio thread, 17 October 2025](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1979215033172127798)
- Human-recorded voice with an AI voice layer applied afterwards, because pure AI speech sounded "robotic or overly polished". Source: [Superside, 30 May 2025](https://www.superside.com/blog/how-we-used-ai-for-our-campaign-video)

### Inferences
- Takes per usable shot in the documented jobs work out at roughly 10 to 30 (300 to 400 generations for 15 clips is 20 to 27 each). A 2-minute film with 60 to 80 seconds of generated footage, cut at 2 to 4 seconds a shot, needs about 20 to 35 generated shots, so plan for several hundred video generations plus a few hundred image generations or edits.
- The practical order of work is: lock the look on stills first (cheap to fix), then animate (expensive to fix). Every source that reported trouble traced it to generating video before the stills were locked.
- Lens and light consistency is mostly handled by writing one fixed "look" block (focal length, light source, film stock, grain) and repeating it in every still prompt, then letting the stills carry it into video. No source gave a stronger method.
- Because model line-ups changed several times in 2026 (new leaders in February, May, July and August; one major service shut down), the film should not depend on one tool's special feature without a fallback.

### Gaps
- No neutral survey of professionals on "average generations per shot" or "average shot length in AI films" exists in what was read; the numbers above are single cases.
- Official prompt guides for Seedance 2.5, Wan 3.0 and Gemini Omni Flash were not read (search budget ran out). The Google guide read is for Veo 3.1 (October 2025), which the October 2026 round-up says is no longer Google's recommended default.
- Costs per generation were not researched here; they belong to the tool comparison notes.

## 3. The "double leaves the body" image: how film effects did it, and how AI makers do it now

### Takeaway
Film effects put one person on screen twice with a locked or repeatable camera, two passes by the same actor (or a double with a replaced face) and compositing; the best-known astral look avoided a flat see-through ghost by back-lighting the double and adding a fine particle shimmer. AI makers get the shot from a locked still with image-to-video, a prompt that keeps the body still and names the glow colour and a slow drift, sometimes a start and end frame or a ready-made "soul" preset; no verified recipe was found for a true lookalike double, so plan to composite.

### Cited Findings

**How film and visual effects do it**

- Doctor Strange's astral form (2016): designed as a transparent character who exists in the real world unseen. The solution was back-lighting: "In the end we back-lit everything", because "When our characters were back-lit, you actually got a lot of negative space, which the eye perceives as contrast." A particle system added "a slightly twinkly patina". The studio called it "one of the hardest effects we've had to deal with". Source: [Framestore project page](https://framestore.com/doctorstrange)
- Twin shots in "Sinners" (2025): the simplest were locked-off split screens. The actor played one side, changed costume, then played the other side quickly so the light barely changed; a body double stood in to cast a shadow and speak the other lines; a tennis ball marked the eyeline; a live 50-50 overlay of both takes checked alignment. Moving shots used a motion-control dolly that replays the camera move. Where the two touch or fight, a double was shot and his head replaced with a machine-learning face tool trained on multi-camera captures of the actor. One hand-over shot took 18 takes. Source: [befores & afters, 11 December 2025](https://beforesandafters.com/2025/12/11/the-art-of-twinning/)
- From the same piece: "The art of digital twinning is inherently binary. It either works seamlessly or it doesn't." A face swap alone was usually not enough: whenever more than the shoulders were visible, they chose to "shoot him twice, no matter how hard it was", because body language gives a double away. Source: [befores & afters, 11 December 2025](https://beforesandafters.com/2025/12/11/the-art-of-twinning/)
- "Mickey 17" (2025): the actor played both copies back to back; split screen was preferred wherever possible; a body double with face replacement covered fights and over-the-shoulder shots; motion control was avoided because it restricts performance. The supervisor's rule: "you always want to use a range of tricks, because you don't want people to figure it out." Source: [befores & afters, 13 June 2025](https://beforesandafters.com/2025/06/13/the-art-of-two-mickeys/)
- The see-through ghost is as old as photography: in the early 1860s a photographer made "spirit photographs" by exposing a plate that already held another person's image. Source: [Den of Geek, "A History of Ghosts on Film"](https://www.denofgeek.com/culture/a-history-of-ghosts-on-film/)

**How AI makers do it now**

- A 2026 how-to page for the "soul leaving body" effect gives sample scenes whose shared parts are: a wide shot that shows the room and the body left behind; a body at rest (slumped, motionless or lying still); a translucent double that rises from the chest or hovers above; a named glow colour (blue-white, or a soft blue rim light); wisps or particles trailing; sometimes a faint tether of light between double and body; warm lamp light on the body against the cool double. Its main advice: slow, deliberate movement of the double against a still body reads better than fast motion. One variant has the double snap back into the body, which then jolts upright with a flash. Source: [Morphic resources page, 2026 (vendor, undated)](https://morphic.com/resources/vfx/soul-leaving-body-effect)
- A preset called "Soul Jump" on Higgsfield starts from one input image. Its sample prompt begins "a man stands completely still in a grassy clearing" and describes a slow camera move in while a translucent, glowing pale-blue-and-silver copy of him rises from his chest, his physical body stays grounded, and the background does not change. Source: [Higgsfield preset sample page](https://higgsfield.ai/motion/2650aad0-190e-4574-bde6-8378071a4d7c/b83e29c8-cfad-4ebd-ab9a-b99b296fb4dc)
- Another template, "Soul Ascension", is image-to-video: the glowing soul separates while "a faint energy tether lingers, soft light radiating from within". Source (search snippet, page not opened): [Magic Hour template](https://magichour.ai/templates/details/cmnsdq5350043za0zbzfghfza-soul-ascension)
- Start and end frames can pin the beginning (sleeper alone) and the end (double standing by the bed). Kling's own quick-start says the two frames should be as similar as possible, because big differences "may cause a lens switch" (an unwanted cut). Source (search snippet, page not opened): [Kling quick-start on start and end frames](https://home.kling.ai/quickstart/ai-video-start-end-frames)
- For start and end frames, lighting direction, camera position and room layout must match between the two images, and the middle of the move should be checked for identity drift. Source: [Magic Hour shot design guide, 2 October 2026 (vendor)](https://magichour.ai/blog/first-frame-last-frame-ai-video)
- Reference-based tools limit how many identity anchors you get: Veo 3.1 takes up to 3 reference images; Kling 3.0 does multi-shot storyboards with reference-based generation. Source: [Pinggy round-up, updated 11 October 2026](https://pinggy.io/blog/best_video_generation_ai_models/)

### Inferences
- The film-effects lessons carry straight over to AI: (1) lock the camera for the rise, which also suits image-to-video; (2) keep the sleeper almost still and move only the double; (3) light the double from behind or from within so it has an edge, with a cooler colour than the warm bedside light; (4) add a fine shimmer or particles instead of plain transparency; (5) keep each double shot short and vary the trick from shot to shot.
- The safest build is a composite: generate the bedroom shot of the sleeper as one locked clip, generate the rising double as a separate clip from the same reference image on a black background, then lay the double over the plate in the editor with a screen or add blend, a glow and a soft mask at the chest. This keeps the face under control and lets the timing be tuned. This recipe is my own suggestion based on the split-screen practice above; no source documented it for AI.
- Because the double is plainly unreal (glowing, translucent), it sits on the stylised side of the "imperfect realism" danger zone described in section 1, which lowers the slop risk.
- A draft prompt in the pattern the sources show (my wording, not from a source): "Locked-off wide shot of a dim bedroom at night, warm bedside lamp. A man sleeps on his side, completely still. A translucent copy of him, glowing soft blue-white from within, with fine drifting particles, slowly sits up out of his chest, stands beside the bed and looks back at the sleeper. The sleeper and the room do not change. Slow, calm motion. Ambient noise: quiet room tone, a soft rising shimmer."

## 4. Structure of a great 2-minute demo or launch film, what hackathon judges reward, and films worth studying

### Takeaway
Show the pain or the product inside the first 5 to 15 seconds, keep to one idea, give about half the runtime to the real product working, and close on the change in the person's day; write about 250 to 300 spoken words for 2 minutes, caption everything, and keep music under the voice. Hackathon guides and the one big-company internal rubric found agree that judges reward a working demo, novelty and one memorable moment more than cinematic polish, so the cinematic part should be short and serve the proof.

### Cited Findings

**Structure and timing**

- A 2-minute pitch plan: problem 15 to 20 s ("Make it concrete and human"), solution 15 to 20 s ("One sentence"), live demo 60 to 90 s ("Show the thing working. Walk through the key flow"), impact 10 to 15 s, ask 5 to 10 s. "No rambling intros. No team bios." Source: [Builder Base guide, 30 April 2026](https://intercom.help/builderbase/en/articles/14836128-builder-s-guide-ch-14-crafting-a-demo-that-lands)
- Story-circle plan for a judged app video: open with "Setup + Need" in the first 15 to 20 s (who it is for and the frustration, in one or two moments viewers recognise), then the app on the real device, then progress and why it beats the alternative, then the new normal with a one-line transformation. Hook and proof should land in the first 60 to 90 seconds. "A great Shipaton video is proof first, polish second." Source: [RevenueCat, 5 September 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/how-to-win-shipaton-part-4-pitching-your-app)
- Founders' rules collected by a venture programme: one founder's 2-minute launch video was about half product walkthrough, and his view is "No one gives a shit about a cinematic launch video"; "Communicate the value prop clearly, in words and visually"; "show over tell"; "if you have goosebumps, then your audience will most likely too"; "A launch is a weapon, not the goal." Source: [a16z speedrun, 19 August 2025](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video)
- A studio's pattern from top launch videos: "first 15 seconds decide the outcome"; the product "is visible or named before the viewer can scroll past"; "Length between 30 and 90 seconds" (12 of its top 15); "One clear category or use case"; "Motion graphics decorate, they don't replace"; "Production quality matters less than narrative tightness." Source: [Flowjam, updated 5 June 2026 (vendor)](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/product-launch-video-examples-2026)
- Hook writing: avoid the word "introducing", avoid opening on a logo, and for short adverts land the hook inside 1.5 seconds. Source: [Flowjam, updated 20 October 2026 (vendor)](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/launch-video-scriptwriting-write-yours-in-60-minutes-2026)
- The average viewer decides whether to keep watching within "the first 8 seconds" (attributed to YouTube's creator guidance); write the hook last; a hook should be a promise, a surprising fact or a question. Source: [Swarmify, updated 25 August 2026](https://swarmify.com/blog/script-writing/)

**Voice-over, words, pace**

- Natural narration is 125 to 150 words a minute (voice artists often 130 to 140), so 2 minutes is 250 to 300 words. Source: [Swarmify, updated 25 August 2026](https://swarmify.com/blog/script-writing/)
- Short-advert rule of thumb: about 2 spoken words a second, and read the script aloud to cut filler. Source: [Flowjam, updated 20 October 2026 (vendor)](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/launch-video-scriptwriting-write-yours-in-60-minutes-2026)
- Shot count reference points: 15 clips in a 30-second advert; an official 15-second example split into 4 shots. Sources: [NPR via Texas Public Radio, 23 June 2025](https://www.tpr.org/2025-06-23/an-ai-video-ad-is-making-a-splash-is-it-the-future-of-advertising); [Kling blog, 28 July 2026](https://kling.ai/blog/kling-3-15s-video-narrative-control-duration)

**Captions, on-screen text, music and sound**

- A survey of 5,616 US adults found 69% watch video with the sound off in public places and 25% even in private; captions gave an 8% lift in advert recall on mobile. Source: [Streaming Media, 20 May 2019, on a Verizon Media and Publicis Media study](https://www.streamingmedia.com/Articles/News/Online-Video-News/80-of-Video-Caption-Users-Arent-Hearing-Impaired-Finds-Verizon-131860.aspx)
- Demo finishing: caption everything and clean auto-captions by hand; "a crisp and consistent narrator above a soft and ducked music bed"; trim "every dead second"; avoid a cursor "zipping rapidly across the screen". Suggested lengths: hero cut 60 to 90 s, walkthrough 2 to 4 minutes. Source: [Vidpros, 10 July 2026 (vendor)](https://vidpros.com/product-demo-video/)
- One public hackathon platform's video rules: 2 to 4 minutes, show the product running, cut idle time, do not speed footage up, avoid music and copyrighted audio, narration expected (clear AI voices allowed, plain text-to-speech not). Source: [Devfolio guide](https://guide.devfolio.co/docs/guide/participating-in-hackathons/judging-1)

**What hackathon judges reward**

- A large software company's internal hackathon uses three-minute recorded video demos. Judges score 0 to 10 on creativity (a 10 is "mind-blowing and novel"), craftsmanship ("built, working and polished"), technical complexity and viability (how easily it fits existing features). All employees vote for a People's Choice award. Its own judging hints are written with jokes. Source: [Slack blog, 8 April 2021](https://slack.com/blog/collaboration/lessons-from-five-years-of-hackathons-at-slack)
- Recommended public criteria: technicality, originality, practicality, usability, and "Wow Factor": "Anything that leaves a lasting impression". Source: [Devfolio guide](https://guide.devfolio.co/docs/guide/participating-in-hackathons/judging-1)
- "Production quality does not affect your judging score. Judges are looking for impressive working demos and innovative solutions, not professional video editing or cinematography." Show "actual functionality, not just slides". Source: [AI Tinkerers video guide](https://aitinkerers.org/docs/public/video_production_guide.md?markdown=1)
- "Judges and users don't care that your app has a chart": value over features; a specific story helps a project stick because judges watch many entries; "judges are only required to watch three minutes"; and "avoid unlicensed music or third-party assets". Source: [RevenueCat, 5 September 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/how-to-win-shipaton-part-4-pitching-your-app)
- The video "should be a demo of their hack, not a presentation". Source (search snippet, page not opened): [Major League Hacking judging plan](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/judging-and-submissions/judging-plan)
- A narrow demo that works beats a broad one that crashes. Source: [Builder Base guide, 30 April 2026](https://intercom.help/builderbase/en/articles/14836128-builder-s-guide-ch-14-crafting-a-demo-that-lands)

**Films worth studying, and what each does**

- Anthropic's computer-use launch: an unedited, real end-to-end task with no motion graphics; the agent finishing a real task on screen is the film (77.7M views on X, as reported by the vendor). Source: [Flowjam, updated 5 June 2026 (vendor)](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/product-launch-video-examples-2026)
- Adapt, "Your company's AI computer": names the use in seven words, then shows three real tasks (it positions before it demos). Perplexity's "Personal Computer": the founder names a new category instead of a feature. Reve 2.0: the same prompt side by side in old and new models, so the upgrade is the film. Thinking Machines Lab: understated abstract motion with a manifesto voice-over, which the article says works only with a famous team. Source: [Flowjam, updated 5 June 2026 (vendor)](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/product-launch-video-examples-2026)
- Fundamental's "Shortcut": founder on screen about 15 seconds, then the demo; reported 4.1M impressions; ends with a call to action. Daylight's DC-1: quiet narrator and a "less" theme with tasteful craft. FLORA: opens with a jab at rivals, then a zoomed-in camera follows the mouse through the product. A 23-second sped-up tool demo with no talking, credited to "show over tell". Source: [a16z speedrun, 19 August 2025](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video)
- Kalshi's basketball advert: openly absurd, fast, 15 clips in 30 seconds; the model for comedy that suits AI footage. Source: [NPR via Texas Public Radio, 23 June 2025](https://www.tpr.org/2025-06-23/an-ai-video-ad-is-making-a-splash-is-it-the-future-of-advertising)
- Zevia's "Break From Artificial": shows AI glitches on purpose, then cuts to real people; a model for mixing generated and real footage with a wink. Source: [Make it Pop, 5 January 2026](https://makeitpop.beehiiv.com/p/make-it-pop-09-the-2026-ai-video-playbook-for-brands-how-to-avoid-the-ai-slop-trap)
- Cautionary, and very close to the planned premise: a 2024 Microsoft Copilot advert in which a user asks whether they can be in three meetings at once and the answer is "Watch me." Viewers doubted the feature existed, and one said it implies someone sent an AI to a meeting in their place. A communication professor's criticisms: it treats a symptom ("No one has ever wanted to be in a meeting"), invites more meetings ("why stop at three?"), and people who send stand-ins lose touch with what was said. Source: [Fortune via The Star, 24 June 2024](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2024/06/24/microsofts-copilot-will-let-you-join-three-meetings-at-once-but-experts-say-it-misses-the-point-no-one-has-ever-wanted-to-be-in-a-meeting)
- Cautionary: Google's "Dear Sydney" (pulled August 2024) and Apple's "Crush" (pulled May 2024), both read as AI or technology replacing a human act. Source: [RedShark News, 4 August 2024](https://www.redsharknews.com/another-tech-giant-has-to-pull-a-pro-ai-ad-after-public-backlash)

### Inferences
- A beat sheet that fits the sources for this film (my suggestion): 0:00 to 0:15 magic-school hook, ending on the question "how does she do it?" and the answer; 0:15 to 0:30 the real pain in one recognisable moment (a late-night meeting invite, a full chat queue) and the hero image of the double rising; 0:30 to 1:35 the real product doing three things with the AI label visible each time (attends the meeting, answers a colleague's chat, the morning brief); 1:35 to 1:50 the change (the person rested, in control, reading the brief and correcting one item); 1:50 to 2:00 one line and the name. That keeps about 55% of the runtime on real product footage, which matches the "about half" rule and the 60 to 90 second demo guidance.
- About 260 to 290 spoken words in total, and less if the hook scene carries dialogue, since the demo needs breathing room for viewers to read the screen.
- Roughly 30 to 45 shots: 12 to 18 generated shots of 2 to 4 seconds in the cinematic parts, and longer held shots (4 to 8 seconds) for product screens so text can be read.
- The sources disagree on cinematic polish: founders and hackathon guides say it does not matter, while the "Wow Factor" criterion and the craftsmanship score reward a lasting impression. The way to satisfy both is a short cinematic hook that sets up the one idea, followed by unfaked product proof.
- The 2024 "three meetings at once" advert shows the two traps for this exact premise: viewers disbelieve the capability unless they see it really working, and they bristle at "more meetings". The film should prove the capability with real screens and frame the benefit as sleep and focus, not as attending more.
- Because music is a rights question in almost every rule set read, use licensed or self-made music from the start.

### Gaps
- Only one large technology company's internal judging rubric was found in public (2021). No public source describes how judges at other large companies score demo videos, and none describes the specific event this film is for; its own rules must be read.
- No neutral, methodical study of launch videos (length, style, results) was found. A "250-video analysis" surfaced in search, but its page held no data when opened. View counts above are vendor-reported.
- No source gave measured norms for music level, number of on-screen words per card or caption style for demo films; the caption figures are from a 2019 survey of general video viewing.
- The widely repeated "80% more likely to watch a whole video with captions" figure appeared only in secondary pages and was not verified.

## 5. Blending real product screen recordings with cinematic generated footage

### Takeaway
No authoritative source covers blending AI-generated footage with real screen recordings as such. Usable guidance comes from product-film practice: let real product footage carry the film and motion graphics only decorate, present the screen through smooth zooms with distractions removed (or composited onto a screen inside the scene), and put one grade, one grain and one sound bed over everything so generated shots stop looking "too clean".

### Cited Findings
- "Motion graphics decorate, they don't replace"; real product footage should carry the launch. Source: [Flowjam, updated 5 June 2026 (vendor)](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/product-launch-video-examples-2026)
- One admired launch film used a zoomed-in camera that follows the mouse through the product, which makes a screen recording feel like camera work. Source: [a16z speedrun, 19 August 2025](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video)
- Screen-recording finishing: "focus-led" editing (dim everything except the one panel that matters), smooth zooms to lead the eye, remove browser tabs, notifications and side panels, slow the cursor, zoom 150 to 200% for small screens, and keep a master project file so a changed button can be patched in one scene. Source: [Vidpros, 10 July 2026 (vendor)](https://vidpros.com/product-demo-video/)
- An agency film that mixed AI footage, stock footage and 2D overlays finished with colour grading, film grain, audio balancing and transition fixes, and added grain because AI footage can look "almost too clean". Text builds, transitions and headline reveals were made as 2D overlays in After Effects and Figma. Source: [Superside, 30 May 2025](https://www.superside.com/blog/how-we-used-ai-for-our-campaign-video)
- An AI advert kept generated shots tied to reality by matching every shot to the client's real product photos and blending them in Photoshop before animating. Source: [studio thread, 17 October 2025](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1979215033172127798)
- A hackathon guide asks for the app shown on the actual device, with screen captures or over-the-shoulder shots that make taps and responses clear. Source: [RevenueCat, 5 September 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/how-to-win-shipaton-part-4-pitching-your-app)
- An in-house product-film team shoots live action with blank white screens and composites the interface afterwards; the designer rebuilds the screens in a vector tool and animates them, so footage and interface can be worked on in parallel and updated later. Source (search snippet, page not opened): [Medium, "How we make product films in-house"](https://medium.com/@sofiahoflin/how-we-make-product-films-in-house-1b911ccbec69)
- Another small team filmed hands on a desk and layered that footage, a screen capture of the app and a 3D phone model in After Effects. Source (search snippet, page not opened): [Medium, "Let's Make a Promo"](https://medium.com/@djfumberger/lets-make-a-promo-d32e15d574b7)
- Raw screen recordings can look flat because they show loading states and lag. Source (search snippet, page not opened): [Motion The Agency (vendor)](https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/ways-to-make-a-product-demo-video)
- For generated shots built from a start and an end image, fixed edges in the scene (doorframes, windows, furniture), the camera position and the horizon must match between the two images. Source: [Magic Hour shot design guide, 2 October 2026 (vendor)](https://magichour.ai/blog/first-frame-last-frame-ai-video)

### Inferences
These are my suggestions built on the practice above; no source states them for AI footage.
- Bridge shots: end a generated shot on a laptop or meeting screen in the scene (generated blank or neutral), then push in until the real recording fills the frame. The "blank screen, composite later" practice gives the same hand-off without a hard cut.
- One finish for both worlds: the same colour grade and a light film grain over generated shots and screen recordings, the same typeface and caption style, and one continuous music and room-tone bed. Sound that starts before the picture changes hides the join.
- Borrow the camera language: give screen recordings slow push-ins and eased zooms that match the generated shots' camera speed, and give generated shots the same calm pace, so neither side feels like a different film.
- Carry one visual motif across both: for example, the double's blue-white glow colour used as the colour of the product's "AI" label, cursor highlight and caption accents. The label then links the magic image to the real proof.
- Keep the real product real: do not generate or redraw product screens with AI. Judges reward proof, and the tells of generated interface text would undercut it.

### Gaps
- No maker breakdown was found of a film that cuts between AI-generated cinematic footage and real product recordings. The two "blank screen" sources could not be opened.
- No source gave measured guidance on grain strength, grade matching or frame-rate matching between generated clips (often 24 fps) and screen captures (often 30 or 60 fps).

## 6. Humour and tone: an internal audience, "replaces you" versus "covers for you", and making an AI stand-in feel trustworthy

### Takeaway
Campaigns that showed AI doing the human part for you (a child's fan letter, "stop hiring humans", an AI friend, being "in three meetings at once") drew backlash, while research on AI stand-ins says the trusted framing is: it extends a person's availability, reports back to them, stays visibly labelled and leaves judgment to the human. For colleagues, self-aware humour about meeting overload is safer than humour about fooling people, and an AI-friendly company is the kindest audience but still punishes "AI so I can slack off".

### Cited Findings

**What research on AI stand-ins says**

- Microsoft researchers who built and studied meeting stand-ins for three years ("Dittos") state the framing: such agents "are not intended to replace people, but to extend their availability across time and context." They define them as mimetic (evoke a specific person), socially situated (talk to colleagues who know that person) and reciprocal (the person "is kept informed about what occurred, enabling follow up, repair, and continuation of the relationship"). Source: [Microsoft Research position paper for a CHI 2026 workshop](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/CHI2026Wkshp-AI-mediated-Communication-Dittos-ACM.pdf)
- Presence comes more from voice and wording than from a realistic face: "vocal characteristics and linguistic style can evoke a sense of presence more strongly than visual fidelity alone, even when visual representations are deliberately stylized." Source: [Microsoft Research position paper, 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/CHI2026Wkshp-AI-mediated-Communication-Dittos-ACM.pdf)
- The dilemma: "realistic representations are overwhelmingly preferred by both Sources and collaborators, yet the more convincing a Ditto becomes, the more ethically fraught its use." Colleagues "may place greater trust in a Ditto than in a generic assistant, even when both are generated by the same underlying AI system." Source: [Microsoft Research position paper, 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/CHI2026Wkshp-AI-mediated-Communication-Dittos-ACM.pdf)
- Real failures seen in their lab: stand-ins "confidently stating things the Source would never say, fabricate memories, act as a confidant, and derail meetings". Named risks: "doppelganger anxiety, misrepresentation, and unintended delegation". Source: [Microsoft Research position paper, 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/CHI2026Wkshp-AI-mediated-Communication-Dittos-ACM.pdf)
- In their deployment, mentors used the stand-in's conversations as starting points for their own direct talks, and one mentor turned repeated questions into an FAQ for everyone. Source: [Microsoft Research position paper, 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/CHI2026Wkshp-AI-mediated-Communication-Dittos-ACM.pdf)

**What workers say they accept**

- Survey of 2,950 decision-makers (May to June 2025): 75% are comfortable working alongside AI agents, 30% with being managed by one, and 24% with agents operating in the background without human knowledge. Nearly 90% expect agents to help them get more done, but 48% worry about added pressure and 36% about less human interaction. Source: [Workday press release, 12 August 2025](https://newsroom.workday.com/2025-08-12-New-Workday-Global-Research-AI-Agents-Are-Here-But-Dont-Call-Them-Boss)
- Experiment with 961 US knowledge workers (March to April 2026): a colleague who disclosed AI use on identical work was rated far lazier and was 24 points less likely to be recommended for high-visibility projects. Describing the AI use as supporting the team, not saving personal time, raised effort ratings from 45% to 56%. Where AI use is actively celebrated, the laziness penalty almost disappears. Source: [Atlassian Teamwork Lab, 10 June 2026](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/ai-at-work/new-research-shows-honesty-about-ai-use-at-work-is-backfiring)
- Survey of about 1,000 US workers: 91% expect some disclosure of AI use in work content (51% say always); if AI and a coworker conflict, 72% follow the coworker; workers "do not want it to have the final say in workplace decisions". Source: [CPA Practice Advisor on a Resume Now survey, 11 August 2026](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/08/11/workplace-trust-in-ai-97-of-workers-still-put-human-judgment-first/188330/)
- Employment lawyers' advice for introducing AI at work: "Be open with your communication. For some, AI can be scary", and explain that it should "relieve them of some of the drudgery and repetitive tasks and allow them to focus on the fun and engaging stuff". Source: [Fisher Phillips, 23 October 2024](https://www.fisherphillips.com/print/v2/content/37322/will-new-ai-digital-replica-laws-lead.pdf)

**Framing that backfired**

- "Stop Hiring Humans" billboards (from late 2024), with lines such as "Artisans won't complain about work-life balance", were called a "dystopian nightmare" and one poster was torn down. The founder later said "I actually don't think people should stop hiring humans" and admitted the lines were chosen to "draw eyes". Source: [Futurism, 16 December 2024](https://futurism.com/stop-hiring-humans-ad)
- An AI companion pendant's New York subway campaign (autumn 2025, under $1 million) used lines such as "I'll never bail on our dinner plans". Riders wrote "make a real friend" and "We don't have to accept this future" on the posters. Source: [New York Times via The Star, 8 October 2025](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/10/08/a-debate-about-ai-plays-out-on-new-york-city039s-subway-walls)
- More graffiti from the same campaign: "AI is not your friend", "Talk to a neighbor". Source: [CNN via WRAL, 16 November 2025](https://www.wral.com/how-this-tiny-device-became-a-symbol-for-the-backlash-against-ai/22249314/)
- Google's "Dear Sydney" advert showed a father asking an AI to write his daughter's fan letter. Critics said it replaced the child's own words, one columnist wrote it made her "want to throw a sledgehammer into the television", and Google withdrew it though it "tested well before airing". The writer's summary: "Again, a Big Tech company has misread the room." Source: [RedShark News, 4 August 2024](https://www.redsharknews.com/another-tech-giant-has-to-pull-a-pro-ai-ad-after-public-backlash)

The same expert noted one benefit: notes help people who are conflict-averse or communicate differently to follow up. ### Inferences
- "Covers for you" reads as helpful when four things are on screen: the human chose to send it; the AI says it is an AI, in words, at the start; it defers ("I will take that back to her") instead of deciding; and the human reads the brief next morning and corrects or follows up. These map onto the researchers' "reciprocal" property, the workers' wish that AI not have the final say, and the 91% disclosure expectation.
- It reads as "replaces you" or as creepy when the double fools colleagues, when colleagues are shown not noticing, when the benefit is "attend more meetings", or when the human is shown as absent from relationships. The pendant and fan-letter backlashes were both about AI standing in for a human bond.
- The glowing double is a help here: a stylised, clearly unreal double says "this is an AI standing in", while a perfect human copy says "deception". That matches the finding that voice and wording carry presence more than a realistic face.
- The magic-school joke should land on the tool, not on colleagues: the classmates are curious and a little envious, and the punchline is an ordinary product name, which lowers the stakes. Jokes at the expense of people who distrust AI are risky; one 2026 Super Bowl advert that mocked AI sceptics was called "condescending" in a search snippet (not verified).
- A small, honest failure played for laughs (the double politely saying "I don't know, I'll ask her") would use the lab's real failure list to build trust, where a flawless double invites disbelief.
- For an AI-positive internal audience, the "laziness penalty" should be weak, but the film should still frame the gain as support for the team (colleagues in another time zone get answers) more than personal time saved.

## 7. Legal and policy limits of a wizard-school homage opening, AI tools' rules, company rules, and labelling

### Takeaway
The sources point one way: the idea (a gifted student at a school of magic who is somehow in several classes at once) is free to use, while the named characters, the films' look, the actors' faces, house emblems, the "Time-Turner" name and the music are protected by a mix of copyright, trade mark and publicity rights. A product pitch that borrows them is not a parody in the legal sense, "homage" is not a defence, and "internal only" reduces exposure but is not an exemption; an original magic-school scene with original characters, no franchise names, no actor lookalike and original music is the low-risk version. How a court would treat any one version is fact-specific and unsettled, and this is a report of what sources say, not legal advice.

### Cited Findings

**A. What is protected**

- Names and ideas under copyright: "Copyright does not protect names, titles, slogans, or short phrases", though "these things may be protected as trademarks", and copyright "does not protect ideas, concepts, systems, or methods of doing something." Source: [U.S. Copyright Office FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html)
- Characters: the leading Ninth Circuit test protects a character that has "physical as well as conceptual qualities", is recognisable as the same character whenever it appears, and is "especially distinctive". A "stock character", such as "a magician in standard magician garb", is not protected. Copying a character need not be exact to infringe. Source: [Grossman LLP on DC Comics v. Towle (2015)](https://www.grossmanllp.com/Ninth-Circuit-Upholds-Copyright-Protection-for-Batmobile)
- Costumes, settings and mood: in the Star Trek fan-film case (C.D. Cal., 3 January 2017) the court found protectable, and copied, the distinctive characters and species, costumes (their artistic aspects), settings such as specific planets and ships, plot points and the franchise's mood. Source: [Loeb & Loeb on Paramount v. Axanar](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2017/01/paramount-pictures-corp-v-axanar-productions-inc)
- Who owns what: the author owns the character rights; Warner Bros. owns the film rights and holds trade marks, first filed in 1998, covering HARRY POTTER, other character names, spells, BUTTERBEER and Diagon Alley, and it pursues infringement, coat-tailing and dilution. Source: [Chartered Institute of Trade Mark Attorneys blog, 29 May 2024](https://www.citma.org.uk/resources/harry-potter-and-the-ip-empire-blog.html)
- The hourglass device's name: US trade mark records list TIME-TURNER applications by Warner Bros. Entertainment filed in June 2016 for metal key chains and for watches and jewellery, and HOGWARTS filings back to 1999. Source (search snippet, page not opened): [Justia trade mark listing for Warner Bros. Entertainment](https://trademarks.justia.com/owners/warner-bros-entertainment-inc-21175/page38)
- How the owner enforces: in 2018 Warner Bros. told fan festivals they could not use any character names, locations or objects from the series, which ended character meet-and-greets and named classes. Its statement: it is "always pleased to learn of the enthusiasm of Harry Potter fans, but we are concerned, and do object, when fan gatherings become a vehicle for unauthorized commercial activity." Festivals carried on with generic "wands and wizards" themes, and character costumes worn by fans were still allowed. Source: [Associated Press via FOX45, 17 June 2018](https://foxbaltimore.com/news/entertainment/warner-bros-crackdown-puts-dark-mark-over-potter-festivals-06-17-2018)
- Actors' likeness: California's statute covers "name, voice, signature, photograph, or likeness" used on products or for advertising or promotion. The common-law right is broader: use of identity to the defendant's advantage, "commercially or otherwise", and courts have reached uses that only "evoke a person's identity". Source: [Right of Publicity Roadmap, California](https://www.rightofpublicityroadmap.com/law/California)
- Lookalikes of actors in their famous roles: in Wendt v. Host International the actors who played two sitcom characters sued over robots in themed bars that called the characters to mind, and the Ninth Circuit sent the case to a jury. The lesson drawn by an advertising lawyer: "even if you're not using someone's likeness, you may still be violating that person's right of publicity", and this matters for generative AI advertising, where a creator may not intend to reference a real person. Source: [Frankfurt Kurnit, Advertising Law Updates, 22 May 2025](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102kbtb/thinking-about-george-wendt-actor-and-advertising-law-icon)
- AI lookalikes and sound-alikes: deepfake adverts that copy a celebrity's appearance or voice would likely violate publicity rights by the same reasoning as the earlier sound-alike cases, and the federal Lanham Act "applies to circumstances that imply sponsorship". The doctrine is aimed at commercial uses; creative uses such as parody may fall outside it. Source: [Venable, 17 May 2023](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2023/05/artificial-intelligence-wants-your-name-image)
- Music and dialogue: copying the most iconic line of a film is not safe just because it is a small fraction, and a short, recognisable clip of a song in a commercial advert may defeat fair use. Source: [Troutman Pepper Locke, 14 April 2026](https://www.troutman.com/insights/debunking-myths-every-copyright-defendant-has-told-themselves/)

**B. What parody and homage allow in the United States**

- The Supreme Court's parody case holds that commercial purpose is only one factor and that parody "has to work its way through the relevant factors, and be judged case by case". It is essential for a parodist to use some of a prior work "to create a new one that comments on the original". Source: [U.S. Copyright Office summary of Campbell v. Acuff-Rose (1994)](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/campbell-acuff-1994.pdf)
- The opinion's own limits: parody "needs to mimic an original to make its point", "whereas satire can stand on its own two feet and so requires justification for the very act of borrowing". If the commentary "has no critical bearing on the substance or style of the original", and the borrower "merely uses to get attention or to avoid the drudgery in working up something fresh", the claim to fairness "diminishes accordingly (if it does not vanish)". And using a copyrighted work "to advertise a product, even in a parody, will be entitled to less indulgence under the first factor" than selling a parody for its own sake. Source: [Campbell v. Acuff-Rose, majority opinion, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/92-1292.ZO.html)
- A mash-up is not automatically a parody: the Ninth Circuit (December 2020) held a Dr. Seuss and Star Trek mash-up book was not fair use on all four factors, because it was "merely mimicking Seuss' style without critiquing or commenting on that style", and new content is not a "get-out-of-jail-free card". The trade mark claims were dismissed because the title and style were artistically relevant and not explicitly misleading. Source: [Loeb & Loeb on Dr. Seuss v. ComicMix](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2020/12/dr-seuss-enterprises-lp-v-comicmix-llc)
- A faithful homage is not fair use: the Star Trek fan films set out to "stay true" to the canon, did not parody or criticise it, and were held commercial even though free to watch, because the makers gained crowdfunding and other benefits. All four factors went to the studio. Source: [Loeb & Loeb on Paramount v. Axanar](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2017/01/paramount-pictures-corp-v-axanar-productions-inc)
- Trade mark parody: since June 2023 a parody gets no special protection when the famous mark is used "as a mark" for the user's own goods or services; the ordinary likelihood-of-confusion test applies, though parody may still make confusion less likely. Source: [Cooley on Jack Daniel's v. VIP Products, 9 June 2023](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2023/2023-06-09-supreme-court-ruling-confirms-limits-on-parody-defenses-to-trademark-claims)
- How uncertain it is: "fair use is gloriously, frustratingly, maddeningly uncertain"; "Courts treat commercial use as presumptively unfavorable"; unlicensed use in marketing or internal presentations is infringement; whether a work "is used internally or commercially will affect the analysis"; and in-house rules of thumb can "underscore willfulness". The authors warn against deciding fair use alone without legal review. Source: [Troutman Pepper Locke, 14 April 2026](https://www.troutman.com/insights/debunking-myths-every-copyright-defendant-has-told-themselves/)
- "Internal use" is not a licence: a rights-licensing body calls it "a common misconception" that internal use within an organisation needs no licence, and says showing a video to a group of employees counts as a public performance. Source (search snippet, page not opened): [Copyright Clearance Center blog](https://www.copyright.com/blog/do-i-need-a-license-to-use-a-copyrighted-work-even-if-im-only-using-it-internally-within-my-company/)

**C. An original "school of magic" scene with original characters**

- In the suit claiming the fourth Harry Potter book copied an earlier wizard story (S.D.N.Y., 6 January 2011), the court dismissed the case: both had a wizard hero who enters and wins a competition, but there were "no similarities beyond this level of abstraction", and the "total concept and feel" differed starkly. A thinly drawn character "does not constitute protectible expression". Source: [Loeb & Loeb on Allen v. Scholastic](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2011/01/allen-v-scholastic-inc)
- Case briefs of the same decision say the court treated wizard schools and enchanted transport as stock scenes of the fantasy genre. Source (search snippet, page not opened): [Studicata brief of Allen v. Scholastic](https://www.studicata.com/case-briefs/case/allen-v-scholastic-inc)
- A generic magician is the textbook unprotected stock character. Source: [Grossman LLP on DC Comics v. Towle](https://www.grossmanllp.com/Ninth-Circuit-Upholds-Copyright-Protection-for-Batmobile)
- The rights owner's own 2018 line was drawn at names, locations and objects from the series and at commercial activity; generic magic themes went ahead. Source: [Associated Press via FOX45, 17 June 2018](https://foxbaltimore.com/news/entertainment/warner-bros-crackdown-puts-dark-mark-over-potter-festivals-06-17-2018)

**D. Naming the character in dialogue versus depicting her**

- A name by itself is not protected by copyright but may be a trade mark. Source: [U.S. Copyright Office FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html)
- A lawyer's explainer for filmmakers: nominative use means referring to a mark's owner or goods "without attempting to use the actual mark for its commercial appeal", and simply mentioning or showing a brand in a film does not imply sponsorship. He adds that every case is fact-specific. Source: [The Legal Artist, 3 May 2016](https://www.thelegalartist.com/blog/ask-greg-yes-trademark-fair-use-exists)
- The limit of that doctrine: a book about a famous film monster lost because it used more of the mark than needed and suggested the owner's sponsorship. Source (search snippet, page not opened): [case brief of Toho v. William Morrow](https://www.lexplug.com/casebrief/toho_co_ltd_v_william_morrow_and_co_inc__67eec67cd362b4484eda0773)
- Depicting a recognisable character engages character, costume and setting copyright (sections A and B above) and, if the face evokes the actor, publicity rights. Sources: [Loeb & Loeb on Paramount v. Axanar](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2017/01/paramount-pictures-corp-v-axanar-productions-inc); [Frankfurt Kurnit, 22 May 2025](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102kbtb/thinking-about-george-wendt-actor-and-advertising-law-icon)
- Law-firm guidance on generative tools for marketing: "Avoid putting trademarks, celebrities, or well-known images and characters in the prompt", because such prompts could lead to copyright, publicity or trade mark claims. Source: [Taft, 13 April 2023](https://www.taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/six-best-practices-for-companies-using-generative-ai-like-dall-e-or-chatgpt-for-marketing-purposes/)

**E. How the main AI image and video tools treat these prompts**

Source: [Google Cloud, Veo responsible AI and usage guidelines](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/responsible-ai-and-usage-guidelines)
- Google's policy for all its generative tools bars content that "violates the rights of others, including privacy and intellectual property rights", "impersonating an individual (living or dead) without explicit disclosure, in order to deceive", and "misrepresenting the provenance of generated content by claiming it was created solely by a human, in order to deceive". Source: [Google Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy, last modified 17 December 2024](https://policies.google.com/terms/generative-ai/use-policy)
- A cross-model help page (it read "last updated 9 days ago" on 14 November 2026; it reports observed behaviour, which can change without notice) says: Google's image model is the "most restrictive on the platform" and blocks many brand and character references, even stylistic ones; Veo has the same limits plus extra scrutiny on motion involving real-world franchises; Kling V3 is strict and inconsistent (the same prompt may pass once and fail later); Seedance 2.0 and 2.5 are more permissive for stylised scenes but refuse photos of well-known real people as references and may check the finished video; OpenAI's image model is very strict on real people and more flexible with fictional characters; Flux 2 and Recraft are the most permissive. - Users report Google's "prominent people" filter firing on fictional characters and on a user's own avatar. Source: [Euronews, 17 February 2026](https://euronews.com/next/2026/02/17/bytedance-says-it-will-add-safeguards-to-ai-video-tool-seedance-20-following-hollywood-bac)

- Midjourney is being sued by Disney and Universal (June 2025) and Warner Bros. Discovery (September 2025). The Warner complaint says even a generic prompt such as "classic comic book superhero battle" produced its characters, and that Midjourney removed video protections, calling it an "improvement" with "fewer blocked jobs". **F. What company and hackathon rules usually say**

- One 2026 corporate hackathon's rules: the entry "must be your original, unpublished work"; it must not show any third-party "advertising, slogan, logo, trademark, or branding" or use content "owned by a third party"; no AI tools except the organiser's own; an entry that "infringes or misappropriates any third-party rights ... may be disqualified", and prizes can be reclaimed. Source: [Elastic Sydney Hackathon official rules, March 2026](https://www.elastic.co/agreements/sydney-hackathon-official-rules)
- A standard clause on a large public hackathon platform: videos "must not include third party trademarks, or copyrighted music or other material unless the Entrant has permission". Source (search snippet, page not opened): [Devpost-hosted rules example](https://snapgames.devpost.com/rules)
- Guides for entrants repeat it: "avoid unlicensed music or third-party assets", and "avoid music and copyrighted audio". Sources: [RevenueCat, 5 September 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/how-to-win-shipaton-part-4-pitching-your-app); [Devfolio guide](https://guide.devfolio.co/docs/guide/participating-in-hackathons/judging-1)
- Some events ban or limit generative tools; others require AI-generated work to be acknowledged and credited. Sources: [Elastic rules, March 2026](https://www.elastic.co/agreements/sydney-hackathon-official-rules); (search snippet, page not opened) [an AWS-themed hackathon's terms](https://www.denodo.com/en/page/aws-ai-hackathon-terms-and-conditions)
- Law firms tell companies to have a written internal-use policy for generative AI, to read each tool's terms (some require an AI-generated label), and to keep trade marks, celebrities and well-known characters out of prompts. Source: [Taft, 13 April 2023](https://www.taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/six-best-practices-for-companies-using-generative-ai-like-dall-e-or-chatgpt-for-marketing-purposes/)
- Law-firm advice on likeness releases covers "employees, customers, event attendees and anyone your company photographs, films or records": list the permitted uses, treat creating a digital replica as a separate consent, and do not feed a person's likeness or voice into third-party AI tools without express consent. Its closing line: "do not treat AI likeness rights as an afterthought buried in a standard release." Source: [Lathrop GPM, 17 August 2026](http://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/ai-is-rewriting-the-rules-on-likeness-update-your-contracts-now/)

**G. Labelling norms and laws for AI-generated content, and a realistic AI double of a consenting person**

- European Union, AI Act Article 50, applying from 2 August 2026: those who deploy a system that makes "deep fake" image, audio or video "shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated". For an "evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional" work the duty shrinks to disclosing that such content exists "in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work". The information must be given "at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure". A deep fake is content that "resembles existing persons" (or objects, places, events) and "would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful". Providers of AI systems that talk to people must make sure people are told they are dealing with an AI unless that is obvious. Source: [EU AI Act, Article 50 text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- The European Commission's draft guidelines (8 May 2026), as reported by a law firm: the deepfake rule applies "even without intent to deceive"; a realistic picture of a made-up but natural-looking person counts; "clearly unrealistic content", such as fantasy scenes, flying humans or dragons, "would generally fall outside"; artistic and fictional works get a reduced duty, not a full exemption; agentic AI that acts for a user must identify itself to the people it meets at the point of contact, a note in the terms or a watermark is not enough, and vague words like "assistant" fall short; the guidelines recommend visible plain-language notices, audio cues and persistent visual indicators. Penalties can reach EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover. Final guidelines and a code of practice were still pending, and a proposed delay to 2 December 2026 covered only the machine-readable marking duty on tool providers. Source: [Greenberg Traurig alert, June 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/-/media/files/insights/alerts/2026/06/gt-alert_deepfakes-chatbots-ai-generated-text-eu-commission-details-transparency-obligations-under-the-ai-act.pdf)
- The EU published a voluntary icon set (basic AI, fully AI-generated, partly AI-modified). An icon alone "does not establish legal compliance", labels must be clearly visible, and a barely visible icon, a footer note or a label that flashes briefly does not comply. The article also notes an exception for "closed B2B environments". Source: [Smashing Magazine, 13 August 2026](https://smashingmagazine.com/2026/08/eu-guidelines-ai-labelling/)
- California's AI Transparency Act, in force from 2 August 2026, puts its duties on providers of generative systems with more than one million monthly users: hidden ("latent") provenance data in every AI image, video and audio file, an optional visible ("manifest") label, and a free detection tool. It creates no private right to sue and, as summarised, no direct duty on end users. Amendment bills were pending. Source: [Morgan Lewis, 3 August 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/08/new-california-ai-disclosure-rules-become-operative)
- New York's synthetic performer law (from 9 June 2026) requires a "conspicuous disclosure" in advertisements that use a digitally created human who is not recognisable as an identifiable real performer. A copy of a real person is instead a "digital replica", governed by consent rules: California Labor Code section 927 and a New York law (both from 1 January 2025) void contract terms for a digital replica that lack a "reasonably specific description of the intended use", unless the person had a lawyer or a union, and, per the firm, they apply to any individual hired in New York or California, not just union talent. Tennessee requires an artist's explicit written consent for an AI voice replica. Source: [BakerHostetler, 8 June 2026](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/contracts-consent-and-clones-navigating-requirements-for-ai-generated-advertising/)
- California's definition of a digital replica: "a computer-generated, highly realistic electronic representation that is readily identifiable as the voice or visual likeness of an individual". The contract rule targets replicas used "in place of work the individual otherwise would have performed". Source: [Fisher Phillips, 23 October 2024](https://www.fisherphillips.com/print/v2/content/37322/will-new-ai-digital-replica-laws-lead.pdf)
- A federal likeness bill (the NO FAKES Act) passed the Senate Judiciary Committee by voice vote on 18 June 2026 and had not been enacted in the sources read. It would create a right over digital replicas made without consent, with exceptions for parody and similar speech. Source: [Holland & Knight, 22 June 2026](https://hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/senate-judiciary-committee-advances-legislation-to-protect-name)
- United States federal baseline: there is "no standalone federal AI disclosure statute"; where disclosure is owed (advertising), it "must be clear, conspicuous, and hard to miss". Google, Meta and TikTok read Content Credentials provenance data. Source: [Dynamis LLP, 8 April 2026](https://www.dynamisllp.com/knowledge/ai-disclosure-in-2026-recent-developments-and-practical-steps-for-brands-and-influencers)
- Public platforms' own rules, if the film is ever posted outside: TikTok requires labels on realistic AI content, YouTube requires disclosure of realistic altered or synthetic content, Meta applies labels automatically. ### Inferences
These are my readings of the sources above, not legal conclusions.

- The planned opening, as described, is not a parody in the legal sense. It does not comment on the wizard story; it uses the story as a familiar vehicle to pitch a product. The Supreme Court's own words cover this: borrowing "to get attention" weakens fair use, and use "to advertise a product, even in a parody" gets "less indulgence". The mash-up and fan-film cases went against the borrower on all four factors. So a recognisable version (named character, school, house look, the actress's face, the hourglass prop, the theme music) would rest on weak ground, and "it is a homage" or "it is internal" would not fix that.
- Risk ladder, lowest to highest: (1) an original school of magic with original characters, no franchise names anywhere, original music and designs: low, because the idea and the genre furniture are free; (2) the same scene plus one spoken mention of the famous character's name: still no copying of protected expression, but it turns a trope into an explicit reference and uses a trade-marked name inside a product pitch, which the nominative-use explainer excludes when the mark is used "for its commercial appeal"; (3) a scene that looks like the films (house colours and crests, robes, great-hall look, the time-device prop as designed, the theme): copies protected expression; (4) the same with a face that evokes the actress: adds publicity-rights exposure, and the lookalike cases say intent is not required; (5) clips, stills, music or dialogue from the films: plain copying.
- Naming versus depicting: depicting a recognisable version is riskier on more fronts (character and costume copyright, the actor's identity, tool filters). Naming alone is narrower, but it is not "safe": it removes the argument that the scene is a generic trope, and it ties the product to the brand in the viewer's mind. Doing both is the worst case. The cleanest joke names nothing and lets the audience make the link themselves.
- The specific plot idea, a top student secretly in several classes at once, is an idea. Its famous expression is the named device, its hourglass-necklace design and the surrounding scenes. An original device (a different object, a different look, a different name or none) keeps the idea and drops the expression.
- Practical tool consequences: prompts with franchise or actor names are likely to be refused, to behave inconsistently, or to void a vendor's copyright promise; "in the style of" a studio character can be blocked too. Describing original designs in plain visual terms avoids both the legal and the practical problem. A model may still drift toward the famous look when asked for "a school of magic", as the Warner complaint describes for superheroes, so outputs need a human check for accidental likeness to the films or the actress.
- Realistic AI double of a consenting employee: no source read makes an internal film unlawful or imposes a specific United States labelling duty on it. The norms all point the same way, though: get the person's specific written consent for this use (the law-firm release advice, and the California and New York contract rules if the replica stands in for their work), label AI-made footage visibly at first exposure (the EU standard, which is also what most workers expect), and make the AI stand-in say it is an AI at the point of contact. A glowing, translucent double is "clearly unrealistic" and so sits outside the EU deepfake definition as described in the draft guidelines; a photoreal double in a meeting window would sit inside it wherever EU rules apply.
- The product in the film already follows the strictest reading (always labelled as AI). Showing that label on screen, readable, in every product shot is both the compliance norm and the trust device.
- Company rules: every rule set read asks for original work, no third-party marks or music without permission, and no infringement of third-party rights, with disqualification as the remedy. A recognisable franchise opening would be the single most likely reason for a film to be pulled or disqualified, more likely than any lawsuit.

### Gaps
- How settled: the copyright idea-versus-expression rule and the parody-versus-attention-getting distinction are long settled at the level of principle, but every fair use and likelihood-of-confusion outcome is case by case. Publicity rights differ by state. Whether AI tools themselves infringe when they output studio characters is unsettled: the main cases were in mediation and discovery through summer 2026, and no ruling or settlement was found (news after August 2026 was not checked because the search budget ran out).
- No source was found on the specific design of the hourglass necklace as a protected design, or on registrations for the house crests; only the TIME-TURNER word filings surfaced, in a search snippet.
- No test was found of what current tools do with prompts naming this franchise or its actors.
