# Three keys in hand unlock the film

**The agent can make the whole film with what is already on the Mac, plus about $165 to $335 of new spending. No AI video subscription is needed.** Code makes about 60% of the runtime: the product screens, the motion graphics, the layering of effects, the edit, the sound mix and the finishing. Three keys already on the Mac make the rest (a key is a password that lets code use a paid service). The fal key reaches Kling 4.0, a new Chinese video model and the best-supported pick for holding one real face; fal is a pay-per-use service that hosts most leading AI video models. The Google key gives still pictures, cheap video drafts and music. The key for ElevenLabs (an AI voice service) gives calm voices and sound effects. The owner must top up fal by about $250, confirm billing on the Google key with a $100 cap, buy one $25-a-month licence for Remotion (the code-based video tool on the Mac) if the film counts as company work, and hand over a photo kit, written consent, a product login and the hackathon rules. Tools no longer limit the ideas. Four rules do: some models refuse real faces, the famous wizard story is protected, several licences are written for small firms, and AI must be labelled. One caution sits over every pick: **no clip was generated for this research**, so each choice is a ranked best guess until about $40 of tests run on day one. The two riskiest parts are whether the owner's face holds in Kling 4.0 and whether the see-through double looks real.

## Code builds 60% of the film and Kling 4.0 holds the face

The field moved fast in 2026, and older advice is now wrong. Sora (OpenAI's video model) shut down on 24 September and has no replacement ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)). Veo 3.1 (Google's older video model) ranks 25th on Artificial Analysis, an independent site that ranks models by blind votes ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video)). Four model families lead: Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash (Google's newest, released 5 November), Seedance 2.5 (from ByteDance), Wan 3.0 (from Alibaba) and MiniMax H3 (a Chinese model family) ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/introducing-gemini-omni-1-2-flash/), [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/image-to-video)). **The top eight sit within 25 points of each other, inside the margin of error**, so rank should not decide ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video)). Price, rules and face handling should. Kling 4.0 (from the Chinese firm Kuaishou) reached fal on 16 October and is too new to have a ranking. fal's own catalogue shows no new Veo, Sora or Runway entry since mid-August ([fal catalogue, 14 November](../fal-catalogue-2026-11-14.md)).

The plan assumes a film in five beats: 15 seconds of magic school, 15 seconds of late-night pain and the double rising, 65 seconds of the real product at work, 15 seconds of the morning after, and 10 seconds for the name and one line. That shape follows pitch guides, which give 60 to 90 seconds of a 2-minute slot to the working product ([Builder Base](https://intercom.help/builderbase/en/articles/14836128-builder-s-guide-ch-14-crafting-a-demo-that-lands)). The table gives one pick and one fallback for each stage. The reasons follow it.

**The hero shot is built in layers, not asked for in one prompt.** The agent first makes still pictures with Nano Banana Pro: a face sheet of the owner, then the first and last picture of each shot. Stills are cheap to fix. Video is not. In an own-face test of nine image models, Nano Banana Pro kept the tester's likeness in all three pictures, while a rival lost it in two of three ([image model test](https://dantaylorwatt.substack.com/p/which-ai-image-generator-should-i)). Kling 4.0 then animates from one still to the next; in one test it landed on both the first and the last picture it was given ([CellCog test](https://cellcog.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-vs-kling-4-0/)). For the moment the double leaves the body, the agent makes two clips from the same still: the sleeper lying still, and the same man sitting up and walking to the desk. It cuts the moving figure out with a tool on fal and lays it over the sleeper at part strength, with a glow ([fal cut-out tool](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/birefnet/v2/video/api)). The double is then see-through by design, and the two bodies cannot melt into one. That matters, because single prompts tend to merge or copy bodies; prompt writers add lines such as "there is only ever one child" to stop it ([fal prompt examples](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/seedance-2-5-prompt-examples)). Film crews make twin shots the same way, with a locked camera, two passes and layering ([befores & afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2025/12/11/the-art-of-twinning/)). The studio behind the best-known film version of this image lit the double from behind and added a fine shimmer, so that a see-through figure still read clearly ([Framestore](https://framestore.com/doctorstrange)). Guides for the AI version add two tips: keep the shot wide, and move the double slowly while the body stays still ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/vfx/soul-leaving-body-effect)). The research found no published test of this effect on any current model. The plan is reasoned, not observed, and the third test below settles it.

**Kling 4.0 is the pick for every shot with the owner's face.** In the one multi-model test with reference pictures it scored 9.0 for keeping a character the same, against 8.5 for Wan 3.0 and 7.5 for Seedance 2.5, and it followed the pictures when pictures and words disagreed ([Atlas Cloud test](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/kling-4-vs-seedance-2-5-vs-wan-3-halloween-fight-test)). A creator used his own face in it on 21 October with no block and no identity check, though the platform he tested on sponsored his video ([creator review](https://moelueker.com/blog/kling-4-review)). Kling's own advice for keeping a face is plain: reuse the same reference pictures, keep each shot to one main action, and build the film shot by shot ([Kling](https://kling.ai/blog/limitations-of-current-ai-video-generation-technology)). Seedance 2.5 is ruled out. It refuses uploads that show real human faces, it has refused AI-made faces too, and in full HD (1080p) it costs about five times as much as Kling 4.0 ([BytePlus](https://ai.byteplus.com/ark/region:ap-southeast-1/docs/create-video-generation-task-api), [Fuser test](https://fuser.studio/articles/kling-4-vs-seedance-2-5), [fal Seedance page](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/us/text-to-video/llms.txt)). Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash is the second model. It costs $0.07 a second at 720p (standard HD) on the Google key, and it leads the ranking for adding visual effects to an existing clip ([Google pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing), [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/video-editing)). But Google's own page says pictures of "certain recognizable people" are not supported, and one developer reported in August that an earlier version blocked footage of his own founder ([Google Omni docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/omni), [developer forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/real-peoples-names-or-likenesses-block-on-own-footage-gemini-omni-flash-preview-skyhill-hq/179433)). So the agent uses it for drafts, for shots with no real face and for effect edits, and tests early whether it takes the owner's face. All the tests cited here are single runs, most by firms that sell access to these models. They show tendencies, not proof.

**The more real the moment, the more it should be real footage.** One playbook for brands puts it bluntly: "The danger zone is imperfect realism" ([Make it Pop](https://makeitpop.beehiiv.com/p/make-it-pop-09-the-2026-ai-video-playbook-for-brands-how-to-avoid-the-ai-slop-trap)). A glowing double is openly unreal, so small flaws are forgiven. A plain shot of the owner waking up is not. The fallback for face shots is therefore a phone, not another model: the owner films himself asleep and waking, with the camera fixed, and the agent matches its colour to the other shots. The owner earlier rejected a cartoon look, which rules out the one style where Seedance would have been usable. The night meeting should also end on the real thing. The agent generates the double at the desk with a blank, glowing laptop screen, then pushes in until the owner's real meeting recording fills the frame. That hand-off is this report's suggestion; no source tested it.

**The opening needs no real face, so it is the easier half.** Kling 4.0 accepts up to seven named subjects and a separate prompt for each shot, which suits one student seen in several classrooms ([fal Kling guide](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/how-to-use-kling-4-0)). Wan 3.0 is the fallback at $0.10 a second at 720p, against $0.157 for Kling 4.0, and it leads the ranking for scenes with several shots ([fal Wan page](https://fal.ai/models/alibaba/wan-3.0/text-to-video/llms.txt), [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video)). Narration should carry the scene. Spoken lines on camera are still the weak point: in a 30-second test one leading model misread a word and another changed a character's voice between lines ([Atlas Cloud test](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/kling-4-vs-seedance-2-5-vs-wan-3-halloween-fight-test)). Whether any model will show the same student twice in one frame is untested. If none does, the agent layers two tries, as it does for the double.

**Half the film is the real product, and that half costs nothing to generate.** The agent drives the product with Playwright (a free tool that works a web browser by script), logs each click and records the page. In Remotion it adds smooth zooms on the clicks, a drawn cursor, a rounded window on a soft background and captions. A retake after a product change is one command. The owner's two existing recordings get the same polish. This is also what wins. A vendor study of 95 enterprise AI launch videos in 2026 found real captured screens had replaced animated mock-ups ([ADVIDS study](https://advids.co/blog/enterprise-ai-launch-video-trends)). One hackathon guide says judges look for "impressive working demos", not cinematography ([AI Tinkerers](https://aitinkerers.org/docs/public/video_production_guide.md?markdown=1)). One trap: logged clicks and video frames can sit about 0.8 seconds apart, so the agent lines them up with a visible flash on the first frame ([XDSoft](https://xdsoft.net/blog/ai-powered-demo-video-recorder)). The fallback is OpenScreen, which has a command line an agent can run and needs a screen-recording permission that a person must grant ([OpenScreen](https://github.com/getopenscreen/openscreen)). Screen Studio, the best-known paid recorder at $29 a month, has no command line, so only a person can use it ([Screen Studio](https://screen.studio)).

**Graphics, voice, music and sound all sit on tools in hand.** Remotion's newest release (4.0.543, 11 November) includes an effects library and 3D support, enough for glass cards and a 3D laptop without extra software ([Remotion releases](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/releases), [Remotion 3D docs](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/three)). ElevenLabs supplies a calm narrator and a separate voice for the product's AI. Under the project's own rule the AI never uses the owner's cloned voice, so no voice cloning is needed. If an AI narrator sounds flat, the owner records the narration on his phone; one agency did that after finding AI voice "still struggles with emotional intonation" ([Superside](https://www.superside.com/blog/how-we-used-ai-for-our-campaign-video)). A 2-minute script is 250 to 300 spoken words ([Swarmify](https://swarmify.com/blog/script-writing/)). Lyria 3.5 costs $0.08 a song on the Google key. ElevenLabs Music costs $0.15 a minute and gives finer control, but its licence is the one trap in this stage, covered below ([Google pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing), [ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)). ffmpeg lowers the music under the voice and sets the final loudness ([ffmpeg](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html)).

**The edit and the finishing run in code too.** Remotion holds the timeline. ffmpeg matches frame rates and colour, removes flicker and adds grain ([ffmpeg](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html)). AI clips get one sharpening pass with Topaz (a tool that sharpens and enlarges video) on fal, at $1.20 per 10 seconds in full HD and $2.60 in 4K ([fal sharpening guide](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/video-to-video-upscalers)). Full HD is the right delivery size. 4K more than doubles that cost, and fal offers Kling 4.0 at 720p and full HD only ([fal Kling page](https://fal.ai/models/kling/v4/text-to-video/llms.txt)). Two details matter. Topaz returns a file type (H.265) that Remotion's recommended video tag does not list, so the agent must change one setting or convert the clips first ([fal Topaz page](https://fal.ai/models/topaz/upscale/video/generative/llms.txt), [Remotion video docs](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/video-tags)). And one colour look plus one light grain over both the generated shots and the screen recordings is what makes them feel like one film; AI footage otherwise looks "almost too clean" ([Superside](https://www.superside.com/blog/how-we-used-ai-for-our-campaign-video)). Nothing in code watches the cut, so the agent must pull a frame at every cut and review them as one sheet. The fallback is DaVinci Resolve Studio, the only editor with an official link to Claude Code, for a last colour pass by eye. It is reported at $295 once and is not installed ([Blackmagic](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/media/partial/release/20260908-03), [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/09/blackmagic-design-releases-resolve-21-1/)).

**Three things still fail often enough to design around.** Spoken lines on camera: mouths now match, but words get misread or clipped, so any on-camera line should be one speaker and a few seconds long ([WaveSpeed guide](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/ai-guides/ai-lip-sync-dialogue-scenes-with-seedance-2-5/)). Long takes: in a 30-second test with an 11-shot brief, the models hit beats early or late, dropped beats or added cuts, so shots should be short and joined in the edit ([Atlas Cloud test](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/kling-4-vs-seedance-2-5-vs-wan-3-halloween-fight-test), [SandBase test](https://blog.sandbase.ai/kling-4-0-vs-seedance-2-5-veo-3-1-2026/)). Small text: unscripted background signs came out as nonsense, so no generated shot should carry text that must be read ([SandBase test](https://blog.sandbase.ai/kling-4-0-vs-seedance-2-5-veo-3-1-2026/)).

**Expected total: about $165 to $335.** This is the report's own arithmetic from the cited prices. The film needs about 15 generated shots. With about eight tries a shot, cheap drafts first and only the chosen shots finished in full HD, video comes to roughly $100 to $250. Stills at about $0.15 each at fal's rate, the sharpening pass, music, sound effects and voice add $40 to $60 ([fal pricing](https://fal.ai/pricing), [ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)). The seat adds $25. The worst case is retakes at the level of one famous AI advert, which took 300 to 400 tries to get 15 usable clips ([NPR](https://www.tpr.org/2025-06-23/an-ai-video-ad-is-making-a-splash-is-it-the-future-of-advertising)). If every one of those tries ran on Kling 4.0 in full HD, video alone would reach about $450 and the total about $530.

Prices need one warning. The fal figures are base prices read from fal's own price list on 14 November, and a higher resolution multiplies them. Gemini Omni's base of $0.0273 a second on fal is the 360p price; in full HD it is $0.1229 ([fal catalogue](../fal-catalogue-2026-11-14.md), [fal Omni page](https://fal.ai/models/google/gemini-omni-flash/v1.2/text-to-video/llms.txt)). Seedance 2.5 is priced per unit of computing work, which comes to about $1.16 a second in full HD ([fal Seedance page](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.5/us/text-to-video/llms.txt)). fal's discount on MiniMax H3 Max ends on 15 November, when its base goes from $0.035 to $0.05 a second ([fal H3 Max page](https://fal.ai/minimax-h3-max)).

Time is as tight as money. One test measured 339 seconds for a single 5-second Kling 4.0 clip ([SandBase test](https://blog.sandbase.ai/kling-4-0-vs-seedance-2-5-veo-3-1-2026/)). An account with little past spend runs only two jobs at once, and the limit rises as paid invoices build up ([fal limits](https://fal.ai/docs/documentation/model-apis/concurrency-limits)). So the top-up should happen today. fal does not charge for failed jobs or for time spent in the queue ([fal pricing](https://fal.ai/pricing)).

**What the owner hands over.** First, a photo kit: 15 to 20 well-lit photos (front, three-quarter, side and full body), one 15 to 30 second clip from a fixed camera, and two minutes of clean speech if he narrates. Tool makers ask for sharp photos from several angles, because with a single photo "the model must guess the unseen parts" ([Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/how-to-put-yourself-in-ai-video), [Kling](https://kling.ai/blog/kling-3-subject-binding-character-consistency)). Second, a short written consent for this use, with the AI copy of him named as its own item, as law firms now advise ([Lathrop GPM](http://www.lathropgpm.com/insights/ai-is-rewriting-the-rules-on-likeness-update-your-contracts-now/)). Third, a login to the product so the agent can capture fresh screens. Fourth, the hackathon rules and the company's policies on AI tools and brand. Fifth, approval to update Remotion and add the browser-capture tool, and a spending cap. The agent should call each service with its key and a fixed budget. The sign-in add-ons that some services offer can spend money with no approval step on the server ([fal docs](https://fal.ai/docs/model-apis/mcp)), so they are best left off.

## Four rules limit the ideas more than any tool does

This section reads public sources. It is not legal advice.

### Real faces: one model refuses, one is unclear, one has no written rule

Seedance 2.5 refuses real faces ([BytePlus](https://ai.byteplus.com/ark/region:ap-southeast-1/docs/create-video-generation-task-api)). Google's key blocks "certain recognizable people", and a user reported that an earlier version blocked his own firm's footage ([Google Omni docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/omni), [developer forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/real-peoples-names-or-likenesses-block-on-own-footage-gemini-omni-flash-preview-skyhill-hq/179433)). Google does offer an approved route: a verified avatar (an approved digital copy of him) that the owner records on his phone and then calls up as `@me` in Flow. Google says all users outside the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland can use it ([Flow help](https://support.google.com/labs/answer/17102997)). Flow has no route for code, so the agent would have to click through a browser, and clips there are paid for with plan credits ([Flowith guide](https://flowith.io/blog/gemini-omni-flash-product-vs-api-access-guide/), [Flow credits](https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16526234?hl=en)). Kling 4.0 has accepted real faces in tests but has no written policy, so that can change without notice. Two more risks are specific to this owner. **No test found used a South Asian face.** And an audit of 3,200 pictures found that earlier image models from Google and OpenAI showed a strong "default white" bias in over 96% of results from neutral prompts ([audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12133)). That audit used text alone. With real photos as the start, the likely risk is slow lightening or smoothing as edits pile up, since a face degrades most under repeated edits ([benchmark paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04151)). So the agent should always edit from the original photos, never from its own outputs, and check every take against one approved photo.

### The magic school: the idea is free, the famous version is not

US copyright does not protect ideas ([US Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html)). A court dismissed a copying claim against the fourth book of the famous wizard series because the two wizard stories shared nothing "beyond this level of abstraction" ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2011/01/allen-v-scholastic-inc)). A gifted student at a school of magic who is in several classes at once is an idea. The character and place names, the look of the films (costumes and settings), the music and the actors' faces are protected by a mix of copyright, trade marks and likeness rights ([CITMA](https://www.citma.org.uk/resources/harry-potter-and-the-ip-empire-blog.html), [Loeb & Loeb on costumes and settings](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2017/01/paramount-pictures-corp-v-axanar-productions-inc), [Frankfurt Kurnit](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102kbtb/thinking-about-george-wendt-actor-and-advertising-law-icon)). A product pitch is not a parody in the legal sense. The US Supreme Court said borrowing "to get attention" weakens the defence, and use "to advertise a product, even in a parody" gets less leeway ([Campbell v. Acuff-Rose](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/92-1292.ZO.html)). "Internal only" lowers the exposure but is not an exemption ([Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/debunking-myths-every-copyright-defendant-has-told-themselves/)). The practical danger is nearer than a lawsuit. One 2026 corporate hackathon's rules demand original work, ban third-party marks and content, and allow disqualification ([Elastic rules](https://www.elastic.co/agreements/sydney-hackathon-official-rules)). The tools push the same way: prompts with franchise names are often refused or behave unevenly ([Scenario](https://help.scenario.com/en/articles/content-policy-copyright-what-each-model-allows/)). **The safe version is an original school, invented characters, an original device, original music and no franchise name in the script or the prompts.** A person must also check that no generated face or set drifted toward the films. The audience will make the link on their own.

### Licences: three depend on whose film this is on paper

Remotion is free for individuals and for companies of up to three people. Everyone else needs the $25 seat ([Remotion pricing](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/pricing)). ElevenLabs' music terms limit its self-serve plans to individuals or small firms; the top self-serve tier stops below 50 employees, and only its enterprise music plans carry no size limit ([ElevenLabs music terms](https://elevenlabs.io/music-terms)). The plan name the key reports, "growing_business", is not on the public price list the research read, so the owner must check which tier it is. Lyria has no size rule in what was read, but Google states commercial use plainly only for its Cloud version, not for the key on the Mac ([Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/lyria/lyria-3)). Suno, the best-known AI music tool, has no official route for code ([Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/07/03/suno-is-opening-an-api-partner-program/)). None of the fal model pages read states a licence for the output, and the model makers' own terms were not read. For a film shown once inside a company these gaps are small. They are still gaps.

### Labels and tone: the project's own rule already meets the strictest standard

The product's AI is always shown as a labelled AI and never uses the owner's cloned voice. No source read asks for more than that. Since 2 August 2026 the EU's AI Act requires disclosure of realistic AI copies of real people ([AI Act, Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)). The European Commission's draft guidance says clearly unreal content generally falls outside the rule, and that an AI acting for a person must say so at the point of contact ([Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/-/media/files/insights/alerts/2026/06/gt-alert_deepfakes-chatbots-ai-generated-text-eu-commission-details-transparency-obligations-under-the-ai-act.pdf)). A glowing, see-through double is clearly unreal. A photoreal copy of the owner speaking in a meeting window is not. The US has no federal AI labelling law ([Dynamis](https://www.dynamisllp.com/knowledge/ai-disclosure-in-2026-recent-developments-and-practical-steps-for-brands-and-influencers)). Workers expect labels anyway: 91% in one survey expect some disclosure of AI use in work content ([Resume Now survey](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/08/11/workplace-trust-in-ai-97-of-workers-still-put-human-judgment-first/188330/)). So three things belong on screen: the product's AI label, readable, in every product shot; the AI saying it is an AI at the start of the meeting; and one end line such as "Cinematic scenes made with AI. Product screens are real." Google's video also carries an invisible watermark, which does no harm ([Google Omni docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/omni)).

Tone is the last limit. A 2024 Microsoft advert in which a user asked to be in three meetings at once drew doubt and unease. Viewers did not believe the feature, and disliked the idea of sending an AI in one's place ([Fortune](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2024/06/24/microsofts-copilot-will-let-you-join-three-meetings-at-once-but-experts-say-it-misses-the-point-no-one-has-ever-wanted-to-be-in-a-meeting)). Microsoft's own researchers give the safer frame: a stand-in is there "to extend their availability", and the person is kept informed afterwards ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/CHI2026Wkshp-AI-mediated-Communication-Dittos-ACM.pdf)). In one experiment, describing AI use as support for the team raised effort ratings from 45% to 56% ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/ai-at-work/new-research-shows-honesty-about-ai-use-at-work-is-backfiring)). So the film should show the owner reading the morning brief and correcting one item. It should sell sleep and focus, not more meetings.

## Seven tests on day one prove the risky parts for about $40

| Order | Test shot | What it proves | If it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Face in stills: a face sheet and four hero stills from the owner's photos, in Nano Banana Pro | His likeness and skin tone survive; Google's image model accepts his photos | Train the small face model on fal; if that fails, film him for every face shot |
| 2 | Face in motion: the same 5-second walk from bed to desk, in Kling 4.0 and in Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash | Each tool accepts a real face; the face holds while he moves | If Kling fails: film the owner, both for the plain shots and for the two layered takes of the double. If only Google refuses: keep every face shot on Kling |
| 3 | The rise out of the body, three ways: Kling 4.0 from a first and last still; Gemini Omni adding the double to a clip of the sleeper; two clips layered in code | Two clean bodies, a true see-through double, no smear at the moment of parting | Layering in code is the safety net, since it is see-through by design |
| 4 | The hand-off: the double at the desk with a blank screen, pushing in to the real meeting recording | The join between generated and real footage works; the AI label reads clearly | Cut straight to the full-screen recording |
| 5 | The magic school: one 10-second shot of an original school with one student in two classes, in Kling 4.0 and in Wan 3.0 | No drift toward the famous films; the student matches herself; no refusal | Layer two tries; redesign the school in the stills |
| 6 | Screen polish: 10 seconds of the existing chat recording with zooms, cursor, background and captions | The code-made look is premium enough | OpenScreen, or the owner records by hand in Screen Studio |
| 7 | Voice and music: two narrators, two AI voices, two music beds | The voices are calm and natural to the owner's ear | The owner records the narration himself |

Tests 1 to 3 come before any other spending, because every later shot depends on them. The order runs from the most basic to the most complex. A face that fails in a still will not hold in motion, and a face that fails in motion makes the hero shot pointless. The cost is the report's arithmetic. A 5-second Kling 4.0 try at 720p is about $0.79, so four tries of each test on each route come to about $40 in all ([fal catalogue](../fal-catalogue-2026-11-14.md)). A workable calendar follows: tests on Saturday and Sunday, generation on Monday and Tuesday, edit and sound on Wednesday, and Thursday morning free for fixes. One maker finished a 30-second AI advert in about two days ([NPR](https://www.tpr.org/2025-06-23/an-ai-video-ad-is-making-a-splash-is-it-the-future-of-advertising)). Five days for a film that is half screen recording is realistic, with no slack.

## Conclusion

The limits have moved. A year ago the open question was whether any model could make these shots. Now four model families can, for cents a second, and what binds the film is one man's rights over his face, one studio's rights over its story, three licences and a label. The project's own rule turns out to be its best creative asset. A double that is openly unreal and openly labelled is the version that the law, the trust research and the craft advice all favour. It is also the easiest one to make well.

Two things follow. The scarce input is not money. It is a few hours of the owner's time before Monday: the photo kit, the consent note, the rules, the answers above, and a yes or no on each test. And the plan should not lean on any one model. Kling 4.0 is a month old and unranked, Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash is nine days old and has no independent review, and a market leader shut down this year. Stills first, short shots, the double as a layer and real screens as proof: that method survives a change of model. The picks in this report might not.
